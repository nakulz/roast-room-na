# Books: Shreyas Doshi

Built from the source table and the verified notes in batches 01 to 03. He has published no book.

He put the craft into Coda essays, two long Lenny conversations, and a run of threads. "I was too lazy to write a whole book on the craft of Product Management, so I wrote this thread instead" (S023)

## LNO Framework (Coda essay, 19 November 2024)
- **Core thesis:** In a high-impact job the list is longer than the time, so equal effort on every task is the mistake. Sort work into leverage, neutral, and overhead, and spend perfectionism only on the first.
- **In his own words, the method:** An HBR article on task prioritization by Elizabeth Grace Saunders spurred it. He did not invent the idea that tasks are unequal. Over about a year at Google he turned that idea into LNO: about 10x or 100x for leverage, about 1.1x for neutral, and little return for overhead, with the labels allowed to change by situation. Strategy and the roadmap are the usual leverage tasks. Overhead gets a deliberately bad job.
- **Taught in:** the essay (S003). Also the 2022 Lenny conversation (S001), the February 2022 time-management thread (S010), the effectiveness thread (S021), the June 2021 opportunity-cost thread (S036), and a 2024 short post that rounds neutral to about 1x (S045). He mentions it again at the October 2024 summit (S002).

## Pre-mortems (Coda essay, 17 January 2021)
- **Core thesis:** Assume the launch has already failed, name the causes while you can still act, and leave the meeting with a short owned plan so you are less likely to need an ugly post-mortem.
- **In his own words, the method:** The base is Gary Klein's HBR pre-mortem, which he summarizes as four steps and does not claim. His change is the lexicon. A tiger will hurt you if ignored. A paper tiger is a threat you are not worried about. An elephant is the thing the team is not saying. Run it one to three months before launch. The leader then picks the top 3 to 5 tigers and elephants, keeps the wording, and names one owner each.
- **Taught in:** the essay (S005). The earlier thread, 26 January 2020, is the same meeting with voting and a DRI (S013). Also the 2022 Lenny conversation (S001) and short reminders in S008, S009, S012, S017, S023, and S037.

## Evaluating PM performance (Coda essay, 7 May 2024)
- **Core thesis:** A ladder that ignores product stage rewards tiny metric moves on mature products and punishes the right risks on early ones. Grade insight, execution, and impact for the stage the PM is actually in.
- **In his own words, the method:** He points to Kent Beck's 3X for stages and does not claim it. Agree the next 3, 6, and 9 months up front, including what evidence will count. Leading indicators and lagging indicators are both in the review. He used this at Stripe in the second half of his time there.
- **Taught in:** S006.

## Product leader archetypes (Coda essay, 31 July 2025)
- **Core thesis:** Product leadership is three hats with blind spots, not only good product decisions and a self-managing team. That earlier belief is the one he says he dropped.
- **In his own words, the method:** Operators scale teams and communication and often borrow product depth. Craftspeople stay close to the work and need an execution partner for company process. Visionaries find the new bet and need both of the others to make it real. Use the set when you hire a CPO or choose your own role.
- **Taught in:** S007.

## The art of product management (Lenny's Podcast, 25 August 2022)
- **Core thesis:** A long working session on how he focuses a team: opportunity cost instead of ROI, a rough 60/30/10 time split, LNO, the three levels of product work, and pre-mortems with tigers, paper tigers, and elephants.
- **In his own words, the method:** High-leverage people should stop doing most positive-ROI work. The big-bet slice of time holds one or two initiatives. Execution fires in a strong team are usually strategy, culture, or interpersonal. He also tells the Google ads story: under $100 million was not worth the discussion, the habit he kept was thinking an order of magnitude bigger, and the high bar made Google miss trends.
- **Taught in:** S001.

## Lenny and Friends Summit, live (31 October 2024)
- **Core thesis:** Busyness is mostly scope and bad product decisions. A real strategy makes planning short. Taste is judging before the result, including the beliefs you adopt.
- **In his own words, the method:** He retracts his Google-era line that strategy is useless and execution is the whole job. The change, as he tells it, started circa 2014 at Twitter and got stronger on early products at Stripe. He also argues that most doors that look two-way are one-way for a PM, because the feature brings follow-on debt. LNO is useful, and scope can still overwhelm it. Past about 50 people the job becomes optics, willpower is finite, and he went back to earlier-stage product.
- **Taught in:** S002.

## Main threads

The threads below are the ones a focus decision keeps landing on. Dates are from the source table.

- **Why products fail, the 7 biases (12 July 2020, S034; long version 26 September 2020, S008).** Failure is a decision failure. Focusing illusion, the IKEA effect, rushing the spec, choosing what is easy to execute, using a tool as a hammer, ignoring catastrophe, and shaping the plan for the exec. Customer Problems Stack Rank is the test for the first. He says much of it reduces to a line he heard from Mike Seibel.
- **Good PMs, Great PMs (11 April 2020, S015).** Great means a better company trajectory, a strategy that is actually sound, metrics that inform, problems prevented on purpose, and stakeholders used as advisors. He later tells people to reread it for a few months (S038).
- **Prioritization is a strategy problem (24 October 2020, S019).** Four ways the strategy is missing or unused, then themes and a percent split. His own quarter was 60 percent differentiators, 30 percent tech foundation, 10 percent speculative bets. The sheet does not pick the themes.
- **Good strategy, bad strategy (31 March 2020, S035).** Fourteen pairs. Good strategy names non-targets and trade-offs. Bad strategy is a prayer, a laundry list, or a bid for near-term consensus. He says many of the ideas are synthesized from Magretta, Helmer, and Rumelt.
- **What is not a strategy (6 June 2020, S047) and how he wants the doc (17 May 2020, S048).** Optimism, vision, mission, OKRs, a roadmap, and MVP-and-iterate do not qualify. The doc is 3 to 7 pages, from a team of three or fewer. Rumelt's test: the opposite hypothesis should be a viable strategy for someone else. Weeks of strategy to save years of the wrong build.
- **Impact heuristic (2020 short posts, S009; full thread 22 August 2021, S028).** Outcomes depend on execution, strategy, and market. The 2021 thread sets each factor to zero and refuses both "Execution is everything, Strategy is only for MBAs" (S028) and "Strategy is everything, Execution is for losers" (S028). A coarser 15 October 2020 line ranks market above strategy above execution (S029).
- **Opportunity cost and the nine time principles (29 June 2021, S036; 12 February 2022, S010).** Stop optimizing for positive ROI. Delegate radically once the Eisenhower matrix stalls. Proof-of-worth tasks and placebo productivity are named here.
- **High agency (27 June 2020, S025).** Definition, the talent-by-agency matrix, and the rule that integrity is not tradable. He credits the term to Eric Weinstein in the 2022 conversation (S001).
- **PM leadership and the 10-30-50 PM (26 October 2018, S011).** Product sense is empathy, domain knowledge, and creativity. Be top 10 percent in one PM sense, top 30 in another, top 50 in the third. About three things done flawlessly as an individual, five as a team, seven as a company.
- **Product metrics (12 September 2020, S016).** Six categories, 3 to 5 key metrics, 3 to 5 leading metrics, metrics-informed rather than metrics-driven. The eigenquestion definition is here. The question is Shishir Mehrotra's.
- **Null hypothesis (11 June 2020, S053; 12 June 2020, S052).** B2B: they do not need it, will not pay, will not deploy. Consumer: they would rather be elsewhere and will not find your feature. The one-line B2B version is also in the July 2020 leadership roundup (S012).
- **Preventable Problem Paradox (19 January 2020, S017).** Companies applaud the rescue and ignore the person who stopped the fall. Pre-mortems are one of his three counters.
- **Apple pie positions (23 February 2020 list, S030; definition 23 February 2023, S050).** Safe sentences the room nods at. The 2023 post is the newer definition: context decides, and judgment does not come from a tweet. He also flags "trying to find product-market fit" as one of these sentences (S024).
- **Annual planning (6 September 2020, S043).** Have a strategy, skip false precision, resolve the top dependencies, make templates serve the team, and repeat the plan.
- **Eigenquestion example (13 August 2020, S051).** Half the time on the right question. The Netflix illustration is catalog size versus consumption friction. He credits Mehrotra.
- **Agreed Target Quality (2 February 2020, S021).** A shared quality label for eng, design, and PM, learned from Jeff Seibert. The same thread points at LNO and at the 10-30-50 PM.
- **Execution problems that are real (15 October 2020, S029).** Eight genuine execution causes, and the claim that most things called execution problems are strategy, interpersonal, or culture.
- **Proof of Worth (30 May 2020, S033).** Tasks done to earn a seat. Acceptable early. A reason to edit the ladder or leave when they are still required of senior PMs.

## Books he recommends

He names these as reading for product people. The notes give his reason in a phrase. Nothing below is a quotation from the books.

Strategy, which he tells people to read twice (S048): Understanding Michael Porter (Joan Magretta), 7 Powers (Hamilton Helmer), Good Strategy / Bad Strategy (Richard Rumelt), Thinking Strategically. He calls 7 Powers one of the best strategy books for tech products (S048). On 31 March 2020 he says many of his good-strategy lines are drawn from the first three (S035).

For PMs, his July 2020 list, with the 7 Habits of Highly Effective People marked as the one to read two or three times (S032): Super Thinking, The War of Art, 100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People, Alchemy, The Cartoon Guide to Statistics, Understanding Michael Porter, Practical Empathy, The Charisma Myth, 7 Powers, Getting Things Done, The Art of Thinking Clearly. A December 2020 addition names Creativity, Principles, Deep Work, and The Secrets of Consulting (S032). An October 2020 set is in the thread as images, so the titles are not in the fetched text. He describes them as collaboration patterns, game design, empowerment, and a practical strategy guide (S032).

For seeing how teams actually behave (S008): Are Your Lights On?, Peopleware, The Systems Bible, Games People Play. The line that the team which sees reality best wins is from 15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership, which he also uses for the to-me versus by-me framing of agency (S008, S025).

For managers, an April 2020 list ordered from getting the job, to doing it, to leaving the org better (S039): The Motive (Lencioni), What Got You Here Won't Get You There (Goldsmith), The Charisma Myth (Cabane), Super Thinking (Weinberg and McCann), HBR's 10 Must Reads on Managing People, The Practicing Stoic (Farnsworth), The Advantage (Lencioni). The same shelf shows up in his July 2020 roundup, plus HBR's 10 Must Reads on Managing Yourself (S012).

Decision-making and psychology he points PMs to (S021): Alchemy (Rory Sutherland), Super Thinking, The Art of the Good Life and The Art of Thinking Clearly (Dobelli), The Practicing Stoic (one of his top five), Mistakes Were Made (But Not By Me), The Charisma Myth. The Art of the Good Life is also a favorite in the problem-prevention thread, next to a Dan Heath book on prevention whose title is not in the fetched text (S017).

Older view and newer view on two strategy staples, both as of his 2020 posts, with no later reversal in this corpus: "Crossing the chasm (LOVED it in 2001, feels quite dated now)" (S009). Christensen's books did not resonate when he tried them about a decade before that post, and as of 2020 he had not gone back (S009).

A personal shelf he says shaped his perspective, thinner for a product decision: Power of Now, A New Earth, Solve for Happy, The Courage to be Disliked, Love Yourself, Mark Manson's work, Anthony de Mello's work, and Sum, alongside The Practicing Stoic and The Art of the Good Life (S042). He also says books are not the only way to learn, and that learning how to read them raised his use of them (S032).
