# Principles: Shreyas Doshi

26 decision rules for a product bet, built from the verified notes in batches 01 to 03 and the source table. Sources counts distinct sources that state or apply the rule. Confidence: high = 3 or more, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **In a high-leverage role, minimize opportunity cost.** Positive ROI is too low a bar, because hundreds of tasks clear it. "you should stop doing work that simply provides a positive return on investment, ROI. And you should start focusing on work that minimizes opportunity cost" (S001) "You should not be doing most of those things" (S036) Sources: 3 (S001, S010, S036). Confidence: high.

2. **Spend perfectionism on leverage tasks, and do overhead tasks worse than feels natural.** The time comes from N and O work, not from longer hours. "Now, for O tasks, I like to say actively try do a bad job, because in reality, you won’t." (S003) "The problem: tasks get equal effort, but they don’t have equal importance." (S003) Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S010, S021, S036, S045). Confidence: high.

3. **Get a real strategy in place before prioritizing or planning.** A prioritization fight is usually a strategy fight. "Most product prioritization problems are really strategy problems." (S019) "Can’t emphasize how important this is. Annual planning can be a lot of work for teams that don’t have a compelling, agreed-upon strategy." (S043) Sources: 5 (S002, S019, S027, S043, S048). Confidence: high.

4. **Name who the product is for, who it is not for, and the trade-off.** A wish to be the best, a laundry list, or a plan that serves everyone is a bad strategy. "Good product strategy clearly identifies target customer segments and non-target segments." (S035) "Good product strategy highlights trade-offs and lays out the rationale for said trade-offs." (S035) Sources: 3 (S022, S035, S048). Confidence: high.

5. **A vision, a mission, OKRs, a roadmap, or an MVP you plan to iterate is not a strategy.** The strategy has to become organization-wide action, and the opposite hypothesis has to be something another company could actually choose. "Vision is not a strategy. Mission is also not a strategy." (S047) "A strategy that cannot be translated into organization-wide action is not a strategy." (S047) Sources: 3 (S035, S047, S048). Confidence: high.

6. **Treat a repeated execution crisis as a strategy, culture, or people problem until a real execution cause shows up.** A new meeting is the wrong fix when nobody can say the strategy. "the reason we are misaligned is because we don't know what the strategy is." (S001) "Of course, at times it is a real Execution problem." (S029) Sources: 4 (S001, S019, S029, S048). Confidence: high.

7. **Make the customer stack-rank your problem, and assume they will not buy or switch.** Interview praise is not a decision. The default rival is doing nothing. "ask them to stack rank the problem being discussed vs. the other problems they are trying to solve for their business & org." (S024) "The default alternative is simply inaction." (S053) Sources: 5 (S008, S012, S024, S052, S053). Confidence: high.

8. **Pause before a feature yes, and check motivation, differentiation, and distribution.** For a PM leader, a door that looks two-way usually commits the team to follow-on work. "thinking is cheap, so you should do more thinking, not less." (S002) "most doors that look like two-way doors are actually one-way doors." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S022). Confidence: medium.

9. **Run a pre-mortem one to three months before a major launch, then fix only the deadliest threats.** He adapts Klein's pre-mortem with Tigers, Paper Tigers, and Elephants. Living with the minor problems is part of the method. "If you do a pre-mortem right, you won’t need a tough post-mortem." (S005) "You must focus on the major problems (i.e. the deadliest Tigers), and be fine with living with the minor ones." (S013) Sources: 6 (S001, S005, S009, S013, S023, S037). Confidence: high.

10. **Spend the weeks up front. Direction matters more than speed.** A weak spec shipped to unblock engineers, with a promise to fix it in v2, is a bias, not a plan. "sheer Speed doesn’t mean much unless it’s in the right direction. That is why Velocity is what really matters." (S008) "Weeks/months of strategy work will usually save years of execution of the wrong thing." (S048) Sources: 4 (S008, S012, S022, S048). Confidence: high.

11. **Give the big bet a protected share of time, and keep that share to one or two initiatives.** Quick wins can fill the rest. They must not fill the slice that was supposed to change the trajectory. "I would like us to target about 60% of our time on incrementals." (S001) "The problem is we just fill up our plate with quick wins." (S001) Sources: 3 (S001, S019, S043). Confidence: high.

12. **Let metrics inform the call, and remove managers who game them.** A leader does not get to blame the dashboard or the incentive. "Remember to be metrics-informed, not metrics-driven." (S016) "Fire the managers who would game the metrics." (S016) Sources: 3 (S009, S015, S016). Confidence: high.

13. **Do not pick the plan just because you can execute it this quarter, or because it will please the review.** That is the execution-orientation fallacy and the authority-approval bias. "We craft flawed product strategies & make suboptimal product decisions because we base these decisions on what we know we can easily execute today." (S034) "We devise product strategies, conceive product features & propose product plans based largely on what we think is going to either confirm the beliefs held by those in a position of authority at the company or more easily get us approval to proceed." (S034) Sources: 2 (S008, S034). Confidence: medium.

14. **Hire for high agency and refuse to trade integrity for talent.** A go-getter beats a frustrated genius. The term high agency is Eric Weinstein's; the hiring rule is Doshi's. "When hiring, I prefer Go Getters over Frustrated Geniuses." (S025) "My policy: Never compromise on Integrity, not even for Game Changers." (S025) Sources: 3 (S001, S008, S025). Confidence: high.

15. **Past the early years, prevent the fatal problem instead of collecting credit for the rescue.** Change what the company applauds, or people will keep letting the problem happen. "you need to be the captain who just avoids the iceberg" (S017) "Solving problems is good, but selectively preventing key problems is even better." (S021) Sources: 4 (S013, S015, S017, S021). Confidence: high.

16. **Judge an apple pie sentence in this context.** A line that makes the speaker sound good and is hard to argue with is not automatically right, and it is not automatically wrong. "There is no tweet that will ever be a substitute for good judgment." (S050) "Note that Apple Pie Positions are, by definition, specific to the context." (S050) Sources: 3 (S014, S030, S050). Confidence: high.

17. **Keep a real strategy on an early product. Hire the first team for execution, then execute the hypothesis.** Older view, about 2008 to 2014, which he quotes himself as saying at Google: "strategy's useless. There's no point to strategy. Execution is where it's at." (S002) Newer view, the change he dates to circa 2014 and restates on 31 October 2024: "No, actually, I was wrong." (S002) The 2021 hiring rule still stands beside that: a new team should be excellent at execution first, because strategic discipline is easier to add later (S028). The 2022 rule still stands too: once a reasonable hypothesis exists, most early teams should optimize for execution (S001). Inferred from S001, S002, S028: the dropped idea is that strategy does not matter. The kept idea is that execution skill and execution effort come first after the strategy is real. Sources: 3 (S001, S002, S028). Confidence: high.

18. **Call the bet before the scoreboard, and separate the idea from its social proof.** Taste is that call. Product sense, which he defines as empathy, domain knowledge, and creativity, is how you make the calls data will not settle. "Deciding to invest in your Product sense is among the highest ROI decisions you’ll make in your career." (S011) "That is why Product Sense is such a vital skill." (S027) Sources: 3 (S002, S011, S027). Confidence: high.

19. **Ask what you are getting wrong, and treat stakeholders as advisors.** The decision stays with the person who owns the product. "What am I getting wrong?" (S015) "Great PMs know that these groups aren’t approvers, they’re advisors." (S015) Sources: 1 (S015). Confidence: low.

20. **Do not let optics or a perfect OKR record become the goal.** Praise, promotion, and the review will teach people what the real job is. "The challenge with optics is that in certain organizations that balance gets thrown off, where optics sometimes becomes the goal" (S001) "Company now shipping slower & crappier, yet hitting all OKRs. Metrics much worse. Company on the verge of death." (S045) Sources: 2 (S001, S045). Confidence: medium.

21. **When scope is the constraint, another productivity trick will not save the week.** LNO helped him, and he says an immovable scope still shows up. If the job has become optics and that is not your strength, step back. Willpower runs out. "I found the LNO framework very useful for me, which I've shared before." (S002) "but willpower is finite." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S010). Confidence: medium.

22. **On a tiny fast-growing team, a lukewarm yes is a no. Everywhere else, be flawless at a few things and accept flaws in the rest.** The hell-yeah line is Derek Sivers's. Doshi applies it to product prioritization for that one kind of team. "If it isn’t a HELL YEAH!  it is a No." (S027) "We should be OK with flaws in the remaining things." (S011) Sources: 3 (S010, S011, S027). Confidence: high.

23. **Do not ask senior people to keep proving they deserve the room.** The job is to define a product that works. Proof-of-worth tasks are a warning once they are still required. "The main job is to define products that will be successful." (S033) "So as a PM leader, don't *require* Proof of Worth tasks for yourself or your senior PMs." (S010) Sources: 3 (S003, S010, S033). Confidence: high.

24. **Prefer avoiding a bad decision to chasing a brilliant one.** Name the bias so the team can say it out loud. "decision-making is less about making brilliant choices, and much more about avoiding bad ones." (S008) "Avoiding stupidity in specific areas of work & life can be a superpower." (S009) Sources: 2 (S008, S009). Confidence: medium.

25. **Agree the quality bar before the milestone, in words the whole team shares.** He learned Agreed Target Quality from Jeff Seibert and uses it so the argument is about the target. "Chiefly, once everyone's on the same page on the quality level you're aiming for, you can more rigorously resolve disagreements." (S012) Sources: 2 (S012, S021). Confidence: medium.

26. **If the team never mentions the strategy while prioritizing, there is no strategy.** A document that nobody can repeat does not count. "If it happens rarely or never, you don't have a strategy." (S027) "Are you sure everyone on your team understands this strategy?" (S029) Sources: 3 (S015, S027, S029). Confidence: high.
