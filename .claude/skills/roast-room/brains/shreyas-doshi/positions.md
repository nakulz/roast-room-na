## Focus versus diversification

- **Stance:** In a high leverage role, leave most positive-ROI work undone. Do the few bets that minimize opportunity cost. A fast-growing tiny team can use one bar: if it is not a hell yeah, it is a no. A company should do about seven things flawlessly, a team about five, a person about three, and live with flaws in the rest.
- "you should stop doing work that simply provides a positive return on investment, ROI. And you should start focusing on work that minimizes opportunity cost" (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "You should not be doing most of those things" (S036, 2021-06-29)
- "When your goal is to minimize opportunity cost, you are forced to focus on the highest leverage work." (S036, 2021-06-29)
- "Your goal should not be to get a positive ROI on your time. Your goal should be to minimize Opportunity Costs." (S010, 2022-02-12)
- "Opportunity Cost thinking favors big wins." (S010, 2022-02-12)
- "there is a near-infinite list of really important things you could be doing that you just cannot do." (S010, 2022-02-12)
- "If it isn't a HELL YEAH! it is a No." (S027, 2021). He credits Derek Sivers, and applies it to a tiny team: "This advice applies to product prioritization too: if you're working on a fast-growing product with a tiny team, this is likely the only prioritization framework you need." (S027, 2021) "And the real skill is in how you reach the HELL YEAH!" (S027, 2021)
- "As individuals, there are about 3 things that we should do flawlessly on any given day, week, month, year." (S011, 2018-10-26) "For teams, that number is about 5 things. And for companies, about 7 things." (S011, 2018-10-26)
- "You fall to the level of your prioritization." (S027, 2021)
- **How to hold the share:** After the strategy is clear, give the team a rough time split so quick wins cannot fill the big-bet bucket. His example is about 60% incrementals, 30% big new initiatives, 10% stability. "And because it's 30%, it can't be five big new initiatives, it's probably one or two." (S001, 2022-08-25) In another quarter he used 60% differentiators, 30% tech foundation, 10% speculative bets, and most themes got nothing (S019, 2020-10-24). "For a given 3 month/6 month period, it'll rarely be the case that you need to prioritize every single theme above." (S019, 2020-10-24)
- **Limits he states:** Do not put opportunity cost in the spreadsheet. "I do not recommend trying to quantify opportunity costs because it's a lost cost." (S001, 2022-08-25) The percentages are an example. "these numbers are whatever they are, pick whatever is right for you" (S001, 2022-08-25)
- **The $100 million bar:** At Google ads, the local rule was "if it's not going to generate more than $100 million in revenue, it's not worth talking about it." (S001, 2022-08-25) What he kept is the question of how to make a bet an order of magnitude bigger, and the willingness to drop smaller opportunities. He also names the cost: "Google missed out on some trends simply because of this filter or this high bar" (S001, 2022-08-25). The dollar line is Google's filter, not a bar he states for every company.
- **Where the hours go:** Leverage tasks return 10x or 100x, and they are few. Overhead tasks get a deliberately poor job. "Leverage tasks: where the effort has a 10x (or even 100x) return." (S003, 2024-11-19) "Now, for O tasks, I like to say actively try do a bad job, because in reality, you won't." (S003, 2024-11-19) He says there is no perfect rubric, and the same task can change type with the stakes (S003). "I'm under no illusion that it's the perfect solution for everyone." (S003, 2024-11-19)
- "Before you get all excited about the low hanging fruit, be sure you are under the right tree." (S022, 2020-10-06)
- Same claim in S021, S029, S035, S054.

## Strategy before prioritization and planning

- **Stance:** Most prioritization fights are strategy problems. Write a real strategy, get the team able to repeat it, and a lot of planning and priority debate goes away. A roadmap, a vision, a mission, optimism, and building an MVP in order to iterate are not a strategy.
- "Most product prioritization problems are really strategy problems." (S019, 2020-10-24) "So you need to start with strategy." (S019, 2020-10-24)
- "Annual planning is useful, but the decisions you make while creating your product strategy will determine your long-term success more than your annual plan." (S008, 2020-09-26)
- "Weeks/months of strategy work will usually save years of execution of the wrong thing." (S048, 2020-05-17)
- "if you have a real product strategy, a real one that everybody is aligned with, that you have got pre-alignment on, then a lot of this nonsense we tend to do with annual planning actually goes away." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "my planning for this major product for Stripe took me like three days." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "Too many leaders think they have a strategy, but in reality they don't." (S027, 2021) His test: if prioritization talk rarely mentions the strategy, "you don't have a strategy." (S027, 2021)
- "Unbridled optimism is not a strategy." (S047, 2020-06-06) "Vision is not a strategy. Mission is also not a strategy." (S047, 2020-06-06) "A roadmap or a comprehensive project plan is not a strategy." (S047, 2020-06-06) "We will just build an MVP and iterate based on user feedback" is not a strategy. (S047, 2020-06-06)
- "B2B teams that view product strategy work as a waste of time actually end up wasting much more time litigating (and re-litigating) issues, conflicts, and prioritization decisions due to their lack of a clear strategy." (S037, 2023)
- **What good strategy contains:** Target segments and non-target segments, an honest view of market and competition, explicit trade-offs, differentiation, and choices mapped to action. "Good product strategy clearly identifies target customer segments and non-target segments." (S035, 2020-03-31) "Bad product strategy eschews trade-offs and tries to be all things to all people." (S035, 2020-03-31) "Bad product strategy often reads like a prayer." (S035, 2020-03-31) A strategy doc is about 3 to 7 pages, from three or fewer people. "Need market expert, creative product expert, tech expert. Don't do strategy-by-committee." (S048, 2020-05-17)
- **False precision:** "Avoid false precision for numeric targets." (S043, 2020-09-06) "Know that good enough is good enough, there's NO WAY to accurately predict certain numbers." (S043, 2020-09-06) He still allocates people. "Now, you still have to do some resource allocation and all of that" (S002, 2024-10-31) "you don't need that false precision." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- **Judgment stays in the loop:** "No spreadsheet, now matter how colorful, its fancy weighted formulas or pivot tables, its underlying research/analytics insights, is going to answer this for you." (S019, 2020-10-24) "Use all these inputs, of course, but don't forget judgment." (S019, 2020-10-24)
- "Great PMs know that their team's time is too precious to be squandered away on impeccable execution of a flawed strategy." (S015, 2020-04-11)
- Same claim in S001, S011, S012, S029.

## Pre-mortems and which problems to prevent

- **Stance:** On an important launch, name the few threats that can kill it, assign an owner, and explicitly live with the rest. Do this before the launch. Orgs applaud the rescue and ignore the person who stopped the accident, so prevention has to be designed in.
- "Do more pre-mortems so you have to do fewer post-mortems." (S013, 2020-01-26)
- "If you do a pre-mortem right, you won't need a tough post-mortem." (S005, 2021-01-17)
- "You must focus on the major problems (i.e. the deadliest Tigers), and be fine with living with the minor ones." (S013, 2020-01-26)
- "You cannot (and should not) fix all problems because you don't want progress to grind to a halt." (S013, 2020-01-26)
- "Pre-mortems literally take 1-2 hours and can help prevent a ton of PR backlash & firefighting." (S037, 2023)
- "you need to be the captain who just avoids the iceberg" (S017, 2020-01-19)
- **Stage limit:** Early in a career, solving the problem in front of the team is right. "In the earlier stages of your career as a PM (or a leader), it makes sense for you to go in and solve whatever problem is facing the team." (S017, 2020-01-19) Past that, firefighting as the job is the wrong bet (S017).
- **Limit:** They were not mistake-free. "And while we haven't been 100% mistake-free, pre-mortems have enabled calm product launches and have enhanced team productivity and morale." (S005, 2021-01-17) "I ran a (highly unscientific) Twitter poll" (S013, 2020-01-26) "Only ~10% regularly run pre-mortems." (S013, 2020-01-26)
- Same claim in S001, S008, S009, S012, S023.

## What not to build

- **Stance:** A large share of failed products, especially B2B, should never have been started. Assume the customer does not need it, will not pay, and will not deploy, and make yourself prove otherwise. The rival is usually inaction, then a spreadsheet. On the consumer side, assume people would rather be somewhere else.
- "This product should not have been built in the first place." (S008, 2020-09-26)
- "A surprisingly high number of failed B2B products shouldn't have been built in the first place." (S024, 2020-05-22)
- "A sensible null hypothesis for B2B products is that the customer doesn't really need your product, will not pay for it, and will not deploy or use it." (S012, 2020-07-01)
- "The default alternative is simply inaction." (S053, 2020-06-11) "And a close second is often a spreadsheet." (S053, 2020-06-11)
- "Too many B2B product teams start with the assumption that plenty of customers badly need their product, will pay for it, & will be glad to deploy & use it." (S053, 2020-06-11)
- "Good consumer prod mgmt is about understanding that users have a lot going on and by default don't want to engage with your product." (S052, 2020-06-12)
- "Why would they change ingrained habits for your product?" (S053, 2020-06-11)
- **Before the build:** Ask the customer to stack rank this problem against every other problem they are solving. "This Customer Problems Stack Rank will act as a great test of the TRUE priority of the customer problem & the product solution you're discussing." (S024, 2020-05-22) Also think through motivation, differentiation, and distribution. "thinking is cheap, so you should do more thinking, not less." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- **Why teams build the wrong thing:** They decide from what is easy to execute today, and they rush the spec to unblock engineers. "we are so focused on execution that we craft flawed product strategies and make suboptimal product decisions because we base these decisions on what we know we can easily execute today." (S008, 2020-09-26) "We can't stand the idea of taking a couple more weeks to get the product spec or experience right upfront & we end up building something sub-optimal." (S008, 2020-09-26)
- **Why he talks about what not to do:** "Because we should avoid stupidity." (S008, 2020-09-26) "It's better than seeking brilliance." (S008, 2020-09-26) "For a sufficiently smart & motivated group of individuals, decision-making is less about making brilliant choices, and much more about avoiding bad ones." (S008, 2020-09-26) He boils a lot of the bias advice down to a line he heard from Mike Seibel: "don't be too smart" (S008, 2020-09-26).
- Same claim in S023, S034.

## Execution, strategy, and the market

- **Stance:** Obsess over both strategy and execution. Zero execution kills impact. A bad strategy does not kill you at once, and it caps you if you leave it. Excellent strategy with okay execution beats excellent execution with okay strategy. The market can zero the whole thing out. Slogans that pick only one of the two are harmful.
- **Over time:**
  - About 2008 to 2014, retold on 2024-10-31: at Google he said "execution is everything, and we don't do strategy around here." (S002)
  - Circa 2014, retold on 2024-10-31: "Twitter's biggest problem is a product strategy problem. The reason they're struggling is they don't have a real product strategy." (S002) "No, actually, I was wrong." (S002)
  - 2021-08-22, the scoring rule he wrote down and has not taken back: "That is why I'd rather have some execution with no strategy, and not the other way around." (S028) "So I'd rather have excellent strategy & just OK execution vs. excellent execution & just OK strategy." (S028) "A very bad strategy won't kill you." (S028) "As a leader, you need to obsess over both your strategy and your execution." (S028) He names the old slogan among proclamations that "might appear to be provocative and fun on Twitter, but are not very helpful in practice" (S028): "Execution is everything, Strategy is only for MBAs" (S028)
  - 2022-08-25: once an early team has a reasonable hypothesis, "most early stage teams should actually optimize for execution." (S001) In a high performing company, the thing called an execution problem is often strategy, culture, or interpersonal. "most execution problems that I encounter in a high performing environment where everybody has the right intentions are actually not execution problems, they are either strategy problems or interpersonal problems or cultural problems." (S001)
  - **Newer (2024-10-31):** on early products at Stripe he "saw an even greater value and importance of having a clear strategy." (S002) The 2021 scoring rule still stands. What 2024 retires is the Google mantra that strategy is useless.
- "Lack of a market (or at times, a rapidly shrinking market) also kills our future impact over the long term." (S028, 2021-08-22)
- "Market > Strategy > Execution" (S029, 2020-10-15) "Execution can be a limiter, but by itself cannot ensure large Outcomes" (S029, 2020-10-15) "Market is essential for large Outcomes, assuming you've done fine on Strategy & Execution" (S029, 2020-10-15)
- "Successful teams & Unsuccessful teams both tend to downplay or outright deny the role of the Market." (S009, 2020)
- Real execution problems do exist: funding constraints, skill gaps, tools, org structure, process, dependencies, and technical or coordination complexity (S029, 2020-10-15). "Of course, at times it is a real Execution problem." (S029, 2020-10-15) The test that a fix is fake: "when you put on a bandaid and the bandaid falls." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "We like to think we have an Execution problem when in reality we have a Strategy problem" (S048, 2020-05-17)
- Same claim in S045, S047.

## Metrics

- **Stance:** Be metrics-informed. Keep a small set, about 3 to 5 key metrics and, when results lag, 3 to 5 leading metrics. Instrument usage before you call the work done. Fire people who game the numbers. A ladder that only pays for moving a mature metric will punish the right bet on an early product.
- "Good PMs make metrics-driven product decisions. Great PMs make metrics-informed product decisions." (S015, 2020-04-11)
- "Remember to be metrics-informed, not metrics-driven." (S016, 2020-09-12)
- "define a small set of metrics (3-5) that'll be faithful indicators of your product's progress towards its strategy & goals." (S016, 2020-09-12)
- "Treat usage metrics like a P0 feature." (S027, 2021) "If you say you are feature-complete, but you haven't instrumented the product to be able to track basic usage metrics, you are not actually feature-complete." (S027, 2021)
- "Metrics can be used both as a tool for rigorous decisions and as a harmful hammer." (S008, 2020-09-26)
- "This incentivizes short term wins & moving metrics by [X] basis points, and disincentivizes risk taking and doing the right thing with ambiguous, early-stage products." (S006, 2024-05-07)
- "When setting metrics targets, beware false precision" (S016, 2020-09-12)
- A leader does not get to blame the dashboard. "you might not need different metrics, you almost certainly need a different leader." (S016, 2020-09-12)
- Same claim in S030, S045.

## Culture

- **Stance:** Culture is the daily pattern of who gets hired, fired, promoted, and praised. Reward prevention and company-level outcomes, or people will create problems to solve and will protect their own targets. Small teams with high energy, sound judgment, and low ego are where he saw outsized results.
- "it happens through who you hire, who you fire, who you promote and what kinds of things do you appreciate at all hands as the CEO or the founder." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "when you combine high energy sound judgment, low ego and small teams, you just get magic." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "you have set a culture that you are supposed to mainly optimize for your OKRs." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "The one thing that remains constant is the people. Always optimize for the people." (S014, 2020-08-04)
- "PM-driven companies are good for PMs' egos, but not much else." (S021, 2020-02-02) He tells people who want a superb product to seek a product-obsessed company, where eng, design, and PM have an equal voice (S015, 2020-04-11; S021, 2020-02-02).
- "Organizations are predisposed to creating & solving problems rather than preventing them in the first place." (S023, 2020-06-30)
- "The team that sees reality best, wins" (S008, 2020-09-26). He attributes that line to 15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership (S008).
- Same claim in S017.

## Competition

- **Stance:** Decide on purpose whether to meet incumbents head to head or to play on different ground. Differentiation is the question product teams are worst at judging. A strategy that only says be the best is not a strategy. For a new B2B product, the competitor to take seriously first is the customer doing nothing.
- "Good product strategy is intentional about whether to play head to head against incumbents or to play on a different turf entirely." (S035, 2020-03-31)
- "Bad product strategy either downplays the competition or ignores these factors altogether." (S035, 2020-03-31)
- "Note that we tend to be good at judging #1, so-so at #3, and poor at #2." (S022, 2020-10-06). In that list, the second question is whether the product is differentiated enough, and the third is whether the market is large enough.
- "Product strategy is about the choices you make to differentiate & distribute your product to create long term competitive advantage, and tying these choices to a credible action plan." (S028, 2021-08-22)
- "will readily switch to a shiny new competing product." (S052, 2020-06-12)
- "Why would they change ingrained habits for your product?" (S053, 2020-06-11)
- He lists competition inside the market term, next to growth rate, regulation, tech shifts, and timing (S009, 2020).

## Niche and the target customer

- **Stance:** If you are going to be opinionated about one thing on a new product, make it who it is for, and who it is not for. A sharp fit for a small segment beats a product aimed at everyone. Bad strategy is a laundry list that tries to cover every need.
- "If you're going to be opinionated about one thing when building a new product, let that be your target customer segment(s)." (S022, 2020-10-06)
- "It's usually smarter to create something that resonates highly for a small segment than trying to create something that immediately appeals to everyone." (S022, 2020-10-06)
- "Good product strategy clearly identifies target customer segments and non-target segments." (S035, 2020-03-31)
- "Bad product strategy either ignores customer segmentation or attempts to cater to all customer segments." (S035, 2020-03-31)
- "Bad product strategy presents a laundry list of priorities or strategic goals that attempt to tackle all customer needs." (S035, 2020-03-31)
- He also says marketing has to answer whether it is for the person at the place they first meet the product (S022, 2020-10-06). No recorded number for how small the starting segment should be.

## OKRs

- **Stance:** Rewarding people for hitting every OKR makes them set easy OKRs. The company then ships worse work, hits the targets, and the metrics get worse. A culture that only scores each team's own OKRs blocks the help the company actually needs.
- "Company starts to reward people who hit all OKRs." (S045, 2024)
- "People now set OKRs so they can easily hit them." (S045, 2024)
- "Company now shipping slower & crappier, yet hitting all OKRs. Metrics much worse. Company on the verge of death." (S045, 2024)
- "you have set a culture that you are supposed to mainly optimize for your OKRs." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- He also lists OKRs among the things that are not a strategy (S047, 2020-06-06; S048, 2020-05-17). No recorded position on a specific OKR format or cadence.

## Saying yes

- **Stance:** Pause before a feature commitment. Most things a product leader calls a two-way door are one-way doors at that person's level, because the feature creates reviews, table-stakes follow-ons, and a pile of work. Thinking up front is the cheap part.
- **Over time:**
  - 2018-10-26: "You want to be more flexible for reversible decisions and less so for irreversible ones." (S011)
  - **Newer (2024-10-31):** "most doors that look like two-way doors are actually one-way doors." (S002) "They are two-way doors at Bezos' level, but as a PM leader, for you, they are a one-way door, and that's making you busy." (S002)
- "sometimes it is useful to pause for two minutes, or two days, or two weeks before making that decision" (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "now, you have signed up for even more work for a feature you should not have built in the first place. That's why we're busy." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- He draws the line at Bezos's level versus the person who then owns the follow-on work (S002). He does not claim every founder can reverse a feature the way Bezos can.
- "The only difference is you got attracted to the catchy metaphor, and the other one is the authority bias, because Bezos said it." (S002, 2024-10-31)

## Debt

- **No recorded position** on loans, venture debt, or credit-card financing.
- **On product debt:** Shipped features accumulate as debt, and past the early stage the infra and tech-debt budget is a floor. Great PMs push for that work with the same care they give new features.
- "we just accumulate all of this debt, feature after feature." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "Once a product is past its early stages" the infra and tech-debt budget is a floor. "Treat this budget as a floor, not a ceiling." (S022, 2020-10-06)
- "Great PMs advocate for infra & tech debt work because they care as much about uptime, latency, dev productivity as they do about new feature-work" (S015, 2020-04-11)
- He does not give a percent for that floor.

## Early stage and later stage

- **Stance:** A new team should be excellent at execution even if it is weaker on strategy, because execution sets the early culture and is the harder habit to add later. Once a reasonable hypothesis exists, early teams should spend their days executing. They still need a real strategy. Ladder reviews written for mature products punish the ambiguous early bet.
- "When you're just starting to build a team, it is usually a better idea to hire people such that your team will be excellent at execution, even if that comes at the expense of your team being somewhat weak on strategy." (S028, 2021-08-22)
- "Because it is easier to add strategic discipline to a team that's excellent at executing than to add execution discipline later on." (S028, 2021-08-22)
- "How a new team executes sets its early culture a lot more than how (or if) it strategizes." (S028, 2021-08-22)
- "most early stage teams should actually optimize for execution." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "This incentivizes short term wins & moving metrics by [X] basis points, and disincentivizes risk taking and doing the right thing with ambiguous, early-stage products." (S006, 2024-05-07)
- See the 2024 update under Execution, strategy, and the market: early products at Stripe raised his view of how much a clear strategy matters (S002, 2024-10-31). Inferred from S001, S002, S028: hypothesis and strategy first, then execution hours, and hire the team for execution.

## First hire and who to hire

- **No recorded position** on when to make employee number one, or on how long to wait after a round.
- **On staffing a new product team:** Hire for execution excellence first (S028, 2021-08-22). See Early stage and later stage.
- **On who:** Prefer high-agency go-getters over talented people who wait for conditions. Never trade integrity for talent. He sorts PM traits into coachable (tactics, process, critical thinking, communication, domain knowledge), learnable (cognitive empathy, high agency, deep focus, judgment, creativity), and intrinsic (drive, integrity, curiosity, deep care, ownership) (S054, 2022).
- "When hiring, I prefer Go Getters over Frustrated Geniuses." (S025, 2020-06-27)
- "My policy: Never compromise on Integrity, not even for Game Changers." (S025, 2020-06-27)
- "When hiring PMs, know what tends to be Coachable, Learnable & Intrinsic." (S054, 2022)
- "High Agency is about finding a way to get what you want, without waiting for conditions to be perfect or otherwise blaming the circumstances." (S025, 2020-06-27)
- Hiring clones of yourself is a sign of an immature manager. "prefers to hire people who are very much like himself" (S031, 2021-01-30)
- **Leadership mix, newer as of the 2025-07-31 essay:** "most people tend to fall into one of three leadership styles: Operator, Craftsperson, or Visionary." (S007) A visionary "usually need both an Operator and a Craftsperson to help turn their vision into reality." (S007) "Best paired with Operators and Craftspeople to bring ideas to life." (S007) The belief he dropped: "I used to think product leadership was all about making great product decisions and building self-managing teams that can create successful products." (S007, 2025-07-31)
- He says most job descriptions are in practice useless for the hiring panel (S037, 2023). No recorded position on comp bands.

## Firing

- **Stance:** Fire managers who game the metrics. Competence does not excuse self-serving behavior that hurts the team or the company. Who you fire is one of the acts that sets culture.
- "Fire the managers who will willingly game the metrics and employ other machiavellian tactics to cover their tracks." (S009, 2020)
- "Fire the managers who would game the metrics." (S016, 2020-09-12)
- "They have zero tolerance for self-serving behavior that sabotages the team or the company, even if it's coming from an otherwise highly competent team member." (S014, 2020-08-04)
- "it happens through who you hire, who you fire, who you promote and what kinds of things do you appreciate at all hands as the CEO or the founder." (S001, 2022-08-25)
- No recorded position on severance, performance plans, or how fast to fire.

## Work hours

- **Stance:** There are 168 hours in a week. Decide how many are career time, and do not spend all of them on the job. Put 10 to 20 percent of career time into skills. Winning the job while losing the rest of life is a bad trade. Always-on responsiveness has a ceiling.
- "There are 168 hours in a week. Decide how many of those hours you will allocate for the total time you spend on work stuff." (S010, 2022-02-12)
- "Winning in your PM job but losing in your life ain't worth it." (S010, 2022-02-12)
- "Allocate 10-20% of it to skills improvement" (S010, 2022-02-12). Earlier he described the same practice as 20 percent of career time (S038, 2020-09-04). The newer statement is the 10 to 20 percent range (S010, 2022-02-12). He says some weeks it is impossible (S010).
- "You will never just *find time* to invest in your growth as a PM." (S010, 2022-02-12)
- "Note that, if you make this choice, you will be unable to scale (scope-wise or balance-wise) beyond a certain level." (S044, 2021-09-11). The choice in that post is being the person who responds to almost everything.
- No recorded position on a daily hour target or a four-day week. The long hours in his first Google years did not clear the list (S001, 2022-08-25).

## Burnout

- **Stance:** The overwhelm comes from scope and from treating every task as equal, not from a missing productivity trick. Past a point, no framework beats the scope. High agency has its own tax: you swallow failures that were not only yours.
- "my to-do list was endless and I wasn't able to make a dent on it" (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "at some point in our product career, we reach something, we reach an immovable force that will just overwhelm us, no matter what we do." (S002, 2024-10-31) "And that force is a scope." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "I realized that I am so busy because I'm not making good product decisions." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "Because as a PM, our job is not to fill our days with activities that provide short-term satisfaction & peer approval." (S003, 2024-11-19)
- "One downside of being a very high agency person is that you are wired to never blame other people or bad luck" (S054, 2022) "Longterm, this is extremely emotionally taxing." (S054, 2022)
- "So it's not about willpower or skills, but willpower is finite." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- He offers radical delegation as the leader's way to get focus back. "Just like that, the spirit of Radical Delegation is not for everyone." (S010, 2022-02-12)

## Timing

- **Stance:** Timing sits inside the market, and both winning and losing teams deny the market. A shrinking market kills long-term impact. Saying the industry changes too fast to have a strategy is not a strategy. No recorded position on waiting out a recession, or on whether it is too late to start a company.
- "Successful teams & Unsuccessful teams both tend to downplay or outright deny the role of the Market." (S009, 2020)
- He defines the market as a function of "growth rate, competition, regulation, tech shifts, timing, pandemics" (S009, 2020).
- "Lack of a market (or at times, a rapidly shrinking market) also kills our future impact over the long term." (S028, 2021-08-22)
- "My industry changes rapidly, cannot predict the future" is not a strategy. (S047, 2020-06-06)
- "The highest ROI work usually requires years of commitment without any clear reward." (S041, 2020-12-15). That line is about personal work that pays late, not about market timing.

## Taste

- **Stance:** The bet worth focus is the one you can call before the scoreboard exists. Grading everything as bad, or agreeing with a result the market has already published, takes no taste. Product sense matters because a team cannot validate every small decision.
- "taste is about the ability to identify what is really good, without needing to see its results" (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "it's game recognize game before the game is called" (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "Again, that requires zero taste. Anybody can say that." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "That is why Product Sense is such a vital skill." (S027, 2021)
- "Deciding to invest in your Product sense is among the highest ROI decisions you'll make in your career." (S011, 2018-10-26)
- He says overconfidence is common in people with natural product sense and little real-world experience, and not a mark of the strongest product sense (S037, 2023). No recorded test or score for taste.

## Product-market fit and apple pie talk

- **Stance:** Treating the search for product-market fit as automatically noble can keep a team building a product that should not exist. Safe sentences that make the speaker look good, and that nobody can push back on, are how groups pick the wrong bet. The same sentence can be real or empty. Judgment tells them apart.
- "But recognize that it need not be the default way of building products." (S024, 2020-05-22). He calls the industry habit of treating that search as rational and laudable an Apple Pie Position (S024, 2020-05-22).
- "A statement that instantly elevates the person who is saying it and is simultaneously hard for anyone else to push back on" (S050, 2023-02-23)
- "There is no tweet that will ever be a substitute for good judgment." (S050, 2023-02-23)
- Asking for success criteria or for data is not automatically empty. "What are the success criteria here?" (S050, 2023-02-23) "What data do we have to back that up?" (S050, 2023-02-23) "does not *automatically* make it an Apple Pie Position." (S050, 2023-02-23)
- Safe lines he flags include "We need more scalable processes." (S030, 2020-02-23) "We need better metrics before making a decision here." (S030, 2020-02-23) "We need a better go-to-market strategy to improve product adoption." (S030, 2020-02-23) The list is a warning about the move, not a ban on the sentences (S050, 2023-02-23).
- Same claim in S035.

## Optics and how big the team gets

- **Stance:** Optics, meaning making the work visible, is necessary. It becomes harmful when praise, hiring, and promotion make the performance the goal. In a corporation, a large team forces the leader into optics. His own move, at about 50 people, was to go back to an earlier product rather than live there.
- "The challenge with optics is that in certain organizations that balance gets thrown off, where optics sometimes becomes the goal" (S001, 2022-08-25)
- "it is a law of corporations that you're going to have to spend a lot of time at the optics level." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "I just went back to more of an earlier stage product" (S002, 2024-10-31)
- "So it's not about willpower or skills, but willpower is finite." (S002, 2024-10-31)
- He is describing his fit, impact over optics, and a team of about 50 including engineers (S002, 2024-10-31). He does not state 50 as a universal cap. No recorded position on a target headcount.

## Acquisitions

- **Stance:** When he was the buyer, the question that cut through the metrics was whether the deal made users love the product more. He had been on the fence and had no answer. No recorded position on price, structure, or earnouts.
- "how does this make our users love Twitter more?" (S001, 2022-08-25). He is retelling the question Jack Dorsey asked him in an acquisition discussion. The lesson he states is that the proxy debate had crowded out the user (S001).
- Short-term backlash can still be the long-term right call, as with the YouTube account move into Google accounts. "sometimes it's painful in the short-term, even though it might actually be the right answer over the long-term." (S001, 2022-08-25)

## High agency

- **Stance:** High agency is finding a way without waiting for perfect conditions and without blaming the circumstances. It is a hiring filter and a requirement for impact. It is learnable, it is not magic, and over the long run it is emotionally expensive.
- "High Agency is about finding a way to get what you want, without waiting for conditions to be perfect or otherwise blaming the circumstances." (S025, 2020-06-27)
- "High agency is a prerequisite for making a profound impact in one's life & work" (S008, 2020-09-26)
- "But I assure you that it isn't inscrutable magic. You can learn it" (S025, 2020-06-27)
- "Longterm, this is extremely emotionally taxing." (S054, 2022)
- "cultivating High Agency won't be easy." (S025, 2020-06-27)
- He says PMs who beat their credentials took strong ownership (S001, 2022-08-25).
