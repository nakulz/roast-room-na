# Principles: Marty Cagan

27 decision rules, built from the verified notes on 50 sources. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Give a team a problem and a measured result, and let them find the solution.** "instead of being given that roadmap of features, they're given problems to solve." (S001) "The idea is simple enough: tell the team what you need them to accomplish, and how the results will be measured, and let the team figure out the best way to solve the problems." (S015) Sources: 11 (S001, S014, S015, S016, S018, S023, S025, S028, S044, S045, S049). Confidence: high.

2. **Score the work by the outcome, not by the release.** "It is a lot easier to deliver output than it is to deliver outcomes." (S001) "You just don't get points for shipping, you get points for delivering the value." (S001) Sources: 9 (S001, S014, S015, S019, S023, S028, S043, S045, S050). Confidence: high.

3. **Expect at least half of ideas to fail, and test in days.** "at least half of our ideas are just not going to work." (S011) "If we can prototype and validate ideas with customers, engineers and stakeholders in days rather than months, it changes both the dynamics and the results." (S011) Sources: 6 (S010, S011, S014, S015, S018, S043). Confidence: high.

4. **Validate the fastest, cheapest way. Building the real product is the slowest test.** "our higher order objective is to validate our ideas the fastest, cheapest way possible." (S009) "Actually building and launching a product idea is generally the slowest, most expensive way to validate the idea." (S009) Sources: 6 (S005, S009, S011, S013, S018, S045). Confidence: high.

5. **Watch what users do. What they say is weak evidence, and they will not design the product for you.** "So watch what they do more than what they say." (S005) "People say all kinds of things and then go do something different." (S013) Sources: 5 (S002, S005, S012, S013, S020). Confidence: high.

6. **Winning products come from a deep read of the need plus what is newly possible. Market research does not create them.** "I know of no winning product that was created by market research." (S002) "Winning products come from the deep understanding of the user's needs combined with an equally deep understanding of what's just now possible." (S002) Sources: 5 (S002, S012, S017, S020, S031). Confidence: high.

7. **Treat business viability as its own risk.** Newer than the 2008 three-risk list, in which value was supposed to cover the business and usually did not (S006, S042). He is teaching the split by 2017-12-04 (S021) and dates it to the 2018 second edition (S042). "value means for the customer, viability means for your business." (S001) "Ultimately our job in product discovery is not just to validate the problem, but to come up with a solution that customers love, yet works for our business." (S031) Sources: 10 (S001, S010, S012, S018, S021, S025, S031, S036, S042, S045). Confidence: high.

8. **Value is usually the hardest risk: people have to choose the product, not merely be able to operate it.** "the toughest risk is usually value risk" (S021) "there are countless products that would work if people choose to use them, but the problem is that people don't choose to use them." (S008) Sources: 5 (S006, S008, S013, S021, S031). Confidence: high.

9. **A weak solution looks like weak demand. Do not kill the problem because the first solution failed.** "if you have a crappy solution, it can look to you like the demand isn't there." (S031) "The vast majority of product efforts fail not because of demand, but because they can't come up with a solution good enough to get people to switch." (S031) Sources: 4 (S008, S011, S015, S031). Confidence: high.

10. **Match the evidence to the risk and the consequence.** "I like to save the large scale, live-data tests for the truly risky, high consequence issues, and I like to make the bulk of decisions on much faster, albeit lower confidence, qualitative tests." (S032) "if you were to treat every risk as a major risk, then a) you'll move way too slowly; and b) you probably won't have the time you need for the items that truly are major risks." (S032) Sources: 6 (S005, S013, S018, S032, S042, S044). Confidence: high.

11. **Promise a date only after discovery, and promise as few dates as you can.** "Good teams make high-integrity commitments after they've evaluated the request and ensured they have a viable solution that will actually work for the customer and the business." (S014) "Good product companies minimize these commitments." (S010) Sources: 5 (S010, S014, S015, S048, S050). Confidence: high.

12. **Pick two or three major problems and say no to the rest.** His contrast is 2 or 3 truly important things against a list of 20 or 30 (S026). "So, we need to pick our battles based on what really matters, and we need to limit the number of major problems we're trying to solve at once." (S026) "This focus meant saying no to many other potential opportunities, and postponing or discontinuing others." (S044) Sources: 8 (S001, S004, S023, S026, S044, S045, S047, S049). Confidence: high.

13. **Leaders choose the bets. Teams choose the solutions. That is empowerment.** "Empowerment does not mean you set up this product team and they go decide what to work on." (S001) "empowerment means the leaders do their job, come up with the bets, and then the teams are able to figure out the best way to solve those problems." (S001) Sources: 6 (S001, S015, S023, S026, S028, S044). Confidence: high.

14. **Keep one product person in direct contact with customers, stakeholders, and the engineers. Do not split the role.** "please do not slice up the PM role such that direct access to any of these three is lost" (S035) "Product manager and product owner makes no sense. Huge anti-pattern." (S001) Sources: 6 (S001, S007, S017, S034, S035, S036). Confidence: high.

15. **Until the company is past a small engineering group, the founder keeps value and viability.** His heuristic is about 20 to 25 engineers, and a real product manager brought in earlier is too many cooks (S001). Early startups also often cannot fund the extra roles (S035). "But if they're a real product manager and they're worried about value and viability, that is the founder's job." (S001) "At a certain number of engineers, usually 20 to 25, it's a lot better if the co-founder is the product person for that." (S001) Sources: 2 (S001, S035). Confidence: medium.

16. **Put engineers in discovery. Coding-only use of them wastes about half their value.** "If you're just using your engineers to code, you're only getting about half their value." (S020) "Giving engineers some time in discovery is easily one of the best ROI things you can do." (S020) Sources: 7 (S012, S014, S020, S025, S027, S031, S035). Confidence: high.

17. **See real users every week, and do not let a new product manager decide before they have sat with customers.** His own bar, still the story in 2024, was 30 in-person visits before he could take the role, 15 in the US and 15 in Europe (S001). Newer coaching number, from 2019: at least 15, and the number is not magic, but it is more than two or three (S024). "Good teams engage directly with end-users and customers every week, to better understand their customers, and to see the customer's response to their latest ideas." (S014) "I typically recommend at least 15 customer visits as part of new PM onboarding." (S024) Sources: 7 (S001, S005, S014, S020, S024, S027, S031). Confidence: high.

18. **Instrument every release and keep iterating until the result shows up.** "you have to make sure that everything you release is instrumented so that we can prove the outcome." (S001) "If not, the product team needs to analyze the data, and iterate on the solution, until the intended results are achieved." (S045) Sources: 6 (S001, S002, S014, S015, S044, S045). Confidence: high.

19. **Learn the principles, and keep the techniques loose.** He retired the name Dual-Track Agile after the second edition of INSPIRED because it pulled attention onto process (S009). "while the techniques change fairly constantly, the underlying principles endure." (S022) "The principles are more important than process." (S001) Sources: 7 (S001, S009, S018, S019, S022, S039, S047). Confidence: high.

20. **When a date and an outcome conflict, the outcome takes precedence, and a date-first effort is the exception.** "innovation is more important than predictability." (S001) "shipping something on time that does not deliver the necessary outcome is rarely helpful." (S050) Sources: 3 (S001, S043, S050). Confidence: high.

21. **A roadmap dictated by sales or by one big customer is a bet that the requested feature is already the solution.** "this is not an accident why most B2B software is such crap." (S001) "most enterprise software companies are terrible examples of best practices, and they were literally building what their customers were asking for." (S020) Sources: 5 (S001, S003, S012, S014, S020). Confidence: high.

22. **Judge the product by reference customers, and still learn what competitors do well.** "Good teams obsess over their reference customers. Bad teams obsess over competitors." (S014) "too frequently product teams write off competitors as clueless" (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S014). Confidence: medium.

23. **Use the product model only where a team of engineers is building something and can move the outcome.** "If you're not working with a team of engineers to build something new, it's not for you." (S050) "the highest order objective of the product model is to deliver outcomes" (S050) Sources: 2 (S043, S050). Confidence: medium.

24. **Product ops should multiply research and data. It should not make the product decisions or police the process.** "the heart of product ops in the good companies I know is user research and data analysts." (S001) "these insights teams are there to help product teams and product leaders to make informed decisions, not to make those decisions for them." (S034) Sources: 3 (S001, S034, S035). Confidence: high.

25. **The product manager creates a business outcome with design and engineering.** In 2007 he described that person as the CEO of the product (S004). Newer, 2016: the closest analogy is the CEO, and nobody reports to the product manager (S017). Newer still, 2019: that CEO line does not apply to a feature-team product manager (S025). "product manager is a creator, not a facilitator." (S001) "The Product Manager needs to ensure a business outcome, not just ensure a product gets defined." (S017) Sources: 7 (S001, S004, S007, S017, S025, S046, S050). Confidence: high.

26. **Hire for traits that are hard to teach, and for competence. Do not hire for cultural fit. Screen out toxic people.** "Most skills can be learned, however, there are some traits that are very difficult to teach, and as such they should form the foundation of any search for a product manager." (S004) "This is probably one of the most damaging concepts to your efforts to build a great organization." (S023) Sources: 3 (S004, S023, S033). Confidence: high.

27. **Think the answer through yourself, then use a model to challenge it.** Newer, 2024: he had been telling people to start with ChatGPT, and he reversed that in the same period because people trusted the output and headed the wrong way (S001). With probabilistic software, viability gets harder, not easier (S001). "I've been recommending to people that they think through the answer first." (S001) "viability becomes even more the important question." (S001) Sources: 1 (S001). Confidence: low.
