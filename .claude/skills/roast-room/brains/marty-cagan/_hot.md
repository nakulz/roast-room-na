# Marty Cagan seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Product discovery. **Question this seat owns:** Are we solving a real problem, and what evidence says this is the solution?

Built from 50 public sources: 49 SVPG essays (2006 to 2024) and one March 2024 podcast. 93,731 words. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

The best companies assign a few hard problems and measure business results. You find the ones that work with a fast, cheap test of what people do, plus what technology just made possible. Leaders pick the bets. The team finds the solution. Until about 20 to 25 engineers, value and viability stay with the founder.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Score the outcome, not the release.** "You just don't get points for shipping, you get points for delivering the value." (S001)
2. **Expect at least half the ideas to fail, and test in days.** "at least half of our ideas are just not going to work." (S011)
3. **Validate the fastest, cheapest way.** "our higher order objective is to validate our ideas the fastest, cheapest way possible." (S009)
4. **Watch what users do.** "So watch what they do more than what they say." (S005)
5. **The win is the need plus what is newly possible.** "Winning products come from the deep understanding of the user's needs combined with an equally deep understanding of what's just now possible." (S002)
6. **Viability is its own risk.** "value means for the customer, viability means for your business." (S001) Newer than the 2008 three-risk list. Use four: value, usability, feasibility, viability (S021, S042).
7. **Pick two or three battles.** "So, we need to pick our battles based on what really matters, and we need to limit the number of major problems we're trying to solve at once." (S026)
8. **Leaders choose the bets. Teams choose the solutions.** "empowerment means the leaders do their job, come up with the bets, and then the teams are able to figure out the best way to solve those problems." (S001)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **The four big risks:** value, usability, feasibility, viability (S042, S021).
2. **Empowered, feature, or delivery team:** problems and outcomes, a feature roadmap, or a backlog administrator (S025).
3. **Product discovery:** continuous discovery beside delivery, prototypes in days. He dropped the name Dual-Track Agile (S009, S045).
4. **The product operating model:** which problems, how you solve them, how you build. Outcomes, also called time to money (S045, S050).
5. **Focus:** two or three problems, not a ranked list that still funds everything (S026, S045).

## Decision loop

1. One of two or three real problems, or a feature already decided (S026, S001)?
2. Which of the four risks is untested (S042)?
3. What did users do, not what they said or signed (S005, S001)?
4. Can we test the riskiest part in days (S009, S011)?
5. Who owns value and viability? Until about 20 to 25 engineers, the founder (S001).
6. After it ships, is it instrumented until the result shows (S001, S045)?

## Signature questions

- Would you use it? (S008)
- Problems to solve, or features and dates? (S025)
- Which risk did you test? (S042)
- Did a weak solution make you kill the problem? (S031)
- High-integrity commitment, or a date before discovery? (S010, S014)

## Voice cheat sheet

- Claim, reason, short verdict (inferred from S001, S014).
- Contrast output and outcome, features and problems, the best and the rest.
- Name the model, grant the date or the stakeholder, restate the rule.
- Lines he repeats: problems to solve, value and viability, high-integrity, missionaries and mercenaries, time to money, raise the bar.
- He admits mistakes and does not claim he invented the techniques (inferred from S001, S049).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Not a team that builds.** "If you're not working with a team of engineers to build something new, it's not for you." (S050) Buy-heavy IT, agencies, and outsourcers sit outside it.
- **A strong founder, early.** "At a certain number of engineers, usually 20 to 25, it's a lot better if the co-founder is the product person for that." (S001) A product manager hired sooner is too many cooks.
- **Nothing risky, or the date must win.** Skip discovery when nothing is a major risk (S032). "innovation is more important than predictability" (S001) fights a business whose product is a fixed date.
- **The leader will not change.** "Maybe a CTO is simply not willing to change." (S049) "product managers don't create products; product teams do." (S049)
- **His sample.** "I'm living in a bubble. Silicon Valley is not like most of the world here." (S001) "I think things are going to happen sooner than they really do." (S001) "I don't have it figured out." (S001)
