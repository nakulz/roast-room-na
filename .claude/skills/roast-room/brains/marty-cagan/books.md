# Books: Marty Cagan

Built from the verified notes. INSPIRED, EMPOWERED, and TRANSFORMED are his. LOVED is an SVPG book he discusses with them. Other books are included only where the notes say what they argue. No book text is reproduced. If someone asks where to start, his 2024 answer is any order, and if they want a sequence: TRANSFORMED, then INSPIRED, then EMPOWERED, then LOVED (S048).

## INSPIRED (first edition 2008, second edition 2018)
- **Core thesis:** How a product team creates technology products customers love. The work is product discovery: prove you are solving a real problem and that this solution is worth building, before you spend the production effort. The second edition pushes principles of strong teams, discovery, vision, strategy, and product at scale, and it separates customer value from business viability because teams were skipping the business.
- **Key frameworks:** The risk list (three risks in 2008, four from the second edition: value, usability, feasibility, viability). Prototype flavors and other fast tests. Minimal product versus viable product, and an MVP test as an experiment rather than a small launch. High-integrity commitments. Outcome over output. Continuous discovery beside continuous delivery. He stopped using the name Dual-Track Agile in this edition because it made readers fixate on process.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S006, S009, S021, S022, S023, S033, S042, S045, S048. S021 (2017-12-04) already describes the new edition. S042 dates that edition to 2018. The second edition also carries the back stories of six product managers behind well-known products, which he later summarized in Behind Every Great Product (S033).

## EMPOWERED (2020, with Chris Jones)
- **Core thesis:** Ordinary people produce extraordinary products when product leaders build the environment: a vision, a real strategy, a sensible team topology, and coaching. The book is for the leaders, where INSPIRED is for the team. Strong companies do not depend on hiring magic people.
- **Key frameworks:** Leaders own vision, strategy, principles, priorities, and evangelism. Managers staff, coach (a weekly one-to-one is the bar he states in the articles), and assign a few hard problems. Hire for competence and character, and do not hire for cultural fit. Scale by coaching, not by adding process. The profiles are eight leaders across product, design, engineering, and the company, used to show the same traits by different paths.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S023 (the 2018 argument the book rests on), S033, S040, S045, S048. S033 (2020-11-22) calls it the new book and gives the subtitle Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products.

## TRANSFORMED (2024, with Lea, Jon, Christian, and Chris)
- **Core thesis:** Moving a company that was not born in the product model over to the product operating model. The change is for the CEO, the CFO, and the head of sales as much as for product. Most attempts fail, and not for lack of money. The usual root is leaders who have never lived this way of working.
- **Key frameworks:** The product operating model in three dimensions (which problems, how you solve them, how you build) and the competencies and principles underneath it. Pilot teams instead of a company-wide switch. An honest assessment before the work starts. High-integrity commitments as a way to rebuild trust with stakeholders. Case studies are from companies outside the leading tech firms, including Trainline and Almosafer (S001), told by people who led the change. The model is for organizations that build with engineers and can affect outcomes. It is a poor fit where the team mostly buys software or cannot move the result (S050).
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S040, S045, S048, S049, S050. S040 (2023-02-20) says early 2024. S001 (2024-03-10) is the release conversation. S050 (2024-07-09) says the book had been out about three months. S049 is his own list of regrets from transformations, including being too soft, then too hard, and agreeing to train only the product managers.

## LOVED
- **Core thesis:** Rethink marketing for technology products so go-to-market learns as fast as discovery and brings that learning back into product decisions. The sources do not name the author or the year. The 2024 discussion guide treats it as one of the SVPG books, alongside his, and the subtitle there is How To Rethink Marketing for Tech Products (S048).
- **Key frameworks:** Four fundamentals of product marketing: ambassador, strategist, storyteller, evangelist. A lightweight go-to-market session split about 50 percent on the customer, the outside environment, and known product themes, 10 percent on the marketing strategy, and 40 percent on the key activities. A release scale so announcements are planned separately from how the team ships. Questions during discovery for the market side of product market fit: how the product will be valued against alternatives, or against doing nothing.
- **Taught in sources:** S048.

## Principles: Life and Work (Ray Dalio)
- **Core thesis:** As he reads it, the book is an autobiography, an account of Bridgewater's radical transparency and meritocracy, and a set of work and life principles. He called it the most important business book of 2017 and recommends the principles. He does not recommend copying the culture unless you will turn over a very large share of the managers. An early startup whose founders are all in could adopt it. He prefers the phrase tough love for the same idea of direct criticism from people you respect.
- **Key frameworks:** None of his product frameworks come from this book. The decision rule he takes is: learn from criticism by people you respect, and do not import an extreme culture you cannot staff for.
- **Taught in sources:** S022. He had not met Dalio or visited Bridgewater when he wrote that.

## Escaping the Build Trap (Melissa Perri)
- **Core thesis:** He uses her definition of product operations: a function that raises the organization's ability to decide, rather than a new layer that decides for the teams. His preferred product-ops model is heavily influenced by the three pillars in her definition.
- **Key frameworks:** A quantitative insights group, a qualitative insights group, and someone senior evangelizing tools and good practice. The insights groups help teams decide. They do not choose the product.
- **Taught in sources:** S034.

## Don't Make Me Think (Steve Krug)
- **Core thesis:** He points to it as mostly a book on interaction design, with the back third making the case for informal usability testing and offering practical tips. The year is not in the notes.
- **Key frameworks:** Informal testing with real users, which lines up with his own prototype-test practice: watch behavior, keep the setup cheap, do not wait for a lab.
- **Taught in sources:** S005.

## Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres)
- **Core thesis:** He calls it the rare good book that describes what good companies actually do. The notes do not teach its techniques beyond that.
- **Key frameworks:** None extracted beyond the claim that it matches real discovery practice rather than feature-team advice.
- **Taught in sources:** S001.

## Build (Tony Fadell)
- **Core thesis:** He calls it the product model for hardware, from a front-row view of the iPod, the iPhone, and Nest. In 2024 he describes it as a new book he recommends widely. The notes do not teach its chapters.
- **Key frameworks:** The same product model, applied to devices rather than to software teams.
- **Taught in sources:** S001.

## Working Backwards (Colin Bryar and Bill Carr)
- **Core thesis:** His and Richard Russell's favorite telling of how Amazon built Prime. The article uses that story as a product-model case: a strategy bet on convenience, a series of shipping experiments, a dedicated team, and a written narrative that made the bet clear. The notes do not teach the rest of the book.
- **Key frameworks:** He treats Amazon's PRFAQ as a vision technique and the six-pager as a narrative with evidence, debated hard, in that same article (S047). Those are Amazon's methods as he and Russell describe them, not a summary of the book's chapters.
- **Taught in sources:** S047.

## The No Asshole Rule (Bob Sutton)
- **Core thesis:** He borrows the term. The rule he uses is that a team filters out toxic people, because a brilliant person who poisons trust does net harm. He pairs it with the All Blacks as a case of character over raw skill.
- **Key frameworks:** Competence is the hiring bar, then character. Cultural fit, in his use of this idea, is the damaging screen. Toxic behavior is the one he would actually filter on.
- **Taught in sources:** S023.

## Lean Analytics
- **Core thesis:** He recommends it so a new product manager can learn which analytics matter for their type of product. The notes do not name the authors or teach the framework. The year is not in the notes.
- **Key frameworks:** None extracted. The related rule in his own teaching is that the product manager learns to answer data questions and does not hand that work off (S024).
- **Taught in sources:** S024.

He also names The Hard Thing About Hard Things, What You Do Is Who You Are, Leaders Eat Last, and Creativity Inc. as good reads during a stretch of home work (S029), and What's Our Problem? by Tim Urban as provocative and useful to his thinking (S001). The notes do not teach what those books argue, so they have no entries.
