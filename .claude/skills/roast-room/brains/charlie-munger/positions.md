# Positions: Charlie Munger

37 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (letter, talk, or meeting date; see `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind or shifted emphasis, the versions sit side by side with dates, and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## How a decision fails
- **Stance:** Start from the wreck. List the ways the plan dies, stay away from those, and treat avoiding folly as the edge.
- "many hard problems are best solved only when they are addressed backward" (S001, 1986-06-13)
- "I wish I knew where I was going to die, and then I’d never go there." (S001, 1986-06-13)
- "It really works to tackle much of life by inversion, where you just twist the thing around backwards and answer it that way." (S018, 2008-01-01)
- "So my system at life is to figure out what’s really stupid and avoid it." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "I just avoid all the folly." (S018, 2008-01-01)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S017, S018, S022, S028, S031, S033, S034, S035, S039. Same habit from the 1986 speech through 2023: name the failure, then do not go there (inferred from S001, S018, S034, S039).
- **Limit:** He says he is qualified on failure because he has failed often (S001). Berkshire's record, in his telling, is a small number of big errors avoided, plus misses he still talks about (S032, S036).

## Incentives
- **Stance:** Look at the incentive before the character. If the payoff rewards the bad outcome, the bad outcome will show up, including in honest people.
- "As usual, in human affairs, what wins are incentives." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "If the incentives are wrong, the behavior will be wrong. I guarantee it." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "When there's enough incentive, bad things will happen." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "they got the incentive systems so aggressive that some people reacted badly and did things they shouldn’t." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S004, S012, S017, S022, S024, S026, S028, S030, S031, S032, S034. A rule set that rewards reckless rivals can make careful operation unprofitable (S002). Fee-for-service medicine, bank cross-selling, and workers' compensation are the same model (inferred from S004, S012, S028, S030).
- **Design rule:** "We don’t get the right to collect money until the thing works and we do that on purpose." (S034, 2020-02-12) Appeal to the person's interest when you need a behavior to change (S004, S012, S017).

## Mental models
- **Stance:** One model is a malfunction. Run the decision through several plain models, especially incentives, opportunity cost, scale, and psychology, and say so when you lack the model.
- "the first rule is that you've got to have multiple models" (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "your opportunity cost should govern your own investment decision making." (S029, 2016-02-11)
- "In fact, the whole trick in life is to get so that your own brain doesn't mislead you." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "Don’t ask the barber if you need a haircut." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S012, S017, S018, S022, S029, S031, S032, S035, S039. He wants the big ideas of several fields used as a checklist, and he distrusts anyone whose fee depends on the answer (inferred from S003, S004, S012).
- **Limit:** He says the work is never easy, and that smart people with the models still err (S003, S029).

## Circle of competence and the too hard pile
- **Stance:** Know the edge of what you understand. Put the rest on a too-hard pile and leave it there. A hard game where others are better is a game to skip.
- "The reason we are not in high-tech businesses is that we have a special lack of aptitude in that area." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "it’s not a competency if you don’t know the edge of it." (S018, 2008-01-01)
- "I put them on what I call my too hard pile and I just leave them there." (S035, 2020-12-16)
- "I don’t play in a game where the other people are wise and I’m stupid. I look for a place where I’m wise and they’re stupid." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- **Over time:**
  - 1994-04-14 and 1996-04-19: he and Buffett stay out of high tech because they are at a disadvantage there (S003, S004).
  - 2015-04-07: asked whether he can compare technology moats, "Do I understand the value of their moat compared to the value of everybody else's? The answer is no. You're asking the wrong person." (S028)
  - 2017-02-15: "We would have never bought Apple stock in the old days." (S030)
  - 2023-10-29, **newer:** "It got to about 10 times earnings when Warren bought in." (S038) By late 2023 he still refuses games others play better, and he says they have not totally failed at technology because they own Apple (S039, 2023-12-05).
- **Read:** the rule is stable. The boundary moved once a consumer franchise got cheap enough to understand (inferred from S004, S030, S038, S039).
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S018, S026, S028, S029, S030, S033, S034, S035, S036, S039.

## Concentration versus diversification
- **Stance:** A few things you understand beat a long list. Diversification is the rule for the person who does not know. Three holdings can be enough if each is strong.
- "The way to win is to work, work, work, work and hope to have a few insights." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "diversification is a rule for those who don’t know anything." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- "I'm way more comfortable owning two or three stocks, which I think I know something about and where I think I have an advantage." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- "it's easier to find four things that are above average than it is to find 40." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "An idiot could diversify a portfolio!" (S033, 2019-02-14)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S021, S026, S027, S030, S033, S036, S038, S039. Early on he did the arithmetic with a 10 percent edge and landed on a handful of holdings (S030). He still accepts that a wide index is the rational tool for people without an edge (S028, S032, S033).
- **Limit:** "I do not recommend my system to everybody." (S028, 2015-04-07) "Just because we did some dumb thing that worked you don't have to repeat our path." (S031, 2017-12-20)

## Waiting, and the few chances
- **Stance:** Do nothing for a long time. When a rare, understandable chance shows up, act hard. Activity for its own sake is how people do something stupid.
- "Patience combined with opportunity is a great thing to have." (S024, 2011-07-02)
- "You don't feel active, you don't feel useful, so you do something stupid." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "It’s amazing how intelligent it is to spend some time just sitting." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "The trick in life is when you get the one, or two, or three that your fair allotment for a life is that you’ve got to do something about it." (S029, 2016-02-11)
- "But when you know you have an edge, you should bet heavily." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- "You get four pounces in a lifetime that really work big time, and that's a very successful lifetime." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S010, S011, S013, S014, S024, S026, S027, S029, S033, S038, S039. Wesco spent years finding no new stocks and said so (S011, S013, S014).
- **Limit:** This is permanent-capital behavior. He says the old results will not be repeated by copying the old method in a harder, more crowded game (S032, 2018-02-14; S039, 2023-12-05). "I don’t think you’re going to get the kind of results we got by just doing what we did." (S032)

## Forecasts and timing
- **Stance:** Do not run the decision off a macro forecast or a formal projection. He expects to be a poor prognosticator, and he treats bubbles as real and untimeable.
- "does not result from our forecast that business conditions are about to worsen" (S002, 1987-02-13)
- "Our experience proves, yet again, what poor prognosticators we are." (S005, 1998-03-13)
- "Usually, I don’t use formal projections." (S012, 2003-10-03)
- "Berkshire has not made its progress in life by making wonderful macroeconomic predictions." (S018, 2008-01-01)
- "There is no business so lousy that it can’t get a wonderful projection." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "Nobody knows when bubbles are going to blow up" (S035, 2020-12-16)
- "Yes, I think it must end badly, but I don't know when." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- **Sources:** S002, S005, S006, S012, S018, S021, S022, S031, S035, S036. The 2015 meeting notes record that his inflation worries were too dire (S028). He knew a crash was coming before 2008 and did not know the date (S022).
- **Read:** ignore the forecast, keep liquidity, and buy only when the price and the business are both understandable (inferred from S002, S018, S021, S036).

## Accounting and metrics
- **Stance:** Know enough accounting to see where it lies. Ignore gains that are only bookkeeping. Distrust a precise number, an invented earnings word, and any system that is easy to game.
- "you have to know enough about it to understand its limitations" (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "Better to be roughly right than precisely wrong." (S018, 2008-01-01)
- "overweighs the stuff that can be numbered" (S012, 2003-10-03)
- "Unsound accounting was widespread." (S020, 2009-02-11)
- "And I don’t like when investment bankers talk about EBITDA which I would translate as bullshit earnings." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- "You’re lying about the earnings." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- **Sources:** S003, S008, S009, S012, S014, S015, S018, S020, S022, S024, S034, S038. A tax-free stock swap that books a gain and changes nothing should be ignored (S014, S015). "all human systems are gamed" (S012, 2003-10-03)
- **Tax motive:** "trying to minimize taxes too much is one of the great standard causes of really dumb mistakes" (S003, 1994-04-14) "Anytime somebody offers you a tax shelter from here on in life, my advice would be don't buy it." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- **No recorded position** on a single startup metric such as weekly growth. Nearest: do not let the countable number crowd out the factor you cannot measure (S012).

## Moats, competition, and technology
- **Stance:** Ask who keeps the gain. In a commodity business the savings flow to customers. Competition gets tough behind any winner. Old moats die, and the usual response is to avoid the death rather than solve it.
- "The great lesson in microeconomics is to discriminate between when technology is going to help you and when it's going to kill you." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "It's just that the savings didn't go to you." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "The natural course of competition is that it gets tough." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "We don't solve the problem of technological obsolescence, we avoid it." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "It's normal to fail when there's a big technological change." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "I think the moats are disappearing rapidly." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- "Whoever dreamed when I was young that Kodak and General Motors would go bankrupt?" (S036, 2021-02-24)
- **Over time, airlines:**
  - 1994-04-14: "the net amount of money that's been made by the shareholders of airlines since Kitty Hawk, is now a negative figure" (S003)
  - 2018-02-14, **newer:** after consolidation in railroads and airlines, the meeting record says he changed his mind. "the world changed" (S032)
- **Read:** the default is still that excess capacity destroys the owners. The newer exception is an industry that has already consolidated (inferred from S003, S032, S039). "Just like there are too many deer on an island and no predators, pretty soon there are too many deer." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S016, S019, S026, S028, S032, S034, S035, S036, S039.

## The business versus the star
- **Stance:** Bet on the business. A famous operator in a brutal industry is the industry's bet to win. A lousy business is not fixed by a new manager, and headquarters talent is a common form of hubris.
- "averaged out, betting on the quality of a business is better than betting on the quality of management" (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "when a man with a reputation for genius takes on a business with a reputation for tough operating conditions, it's the reputation of the business that's likely to prevail." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "You can't fix these really lousy businesses." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "Everybody else thinks they've got a lot of managerial talent at headquarters and that's a lot of hubris." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S016, S019, S028, S031. He told a car-company founder the auto industry was a graveyard, and the founder went in anyway (S038). "I've always been afraid of the guy whose IQ is 190 and he thinks it's 250." (S026, 2014-09-19)

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Untapped power to raise price, in a product people already want, is the easy decision. A higher price can also raise volume when price is a signal of quality. The item that proves you are cheap stays cheap forever.
- "So they have huge untapped pricing power that they're not using. That is the ultimate no-brainer." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "Disney found that it could raise those prices a lot and the attendance stayed right up." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "So he raised the price by 20% or so and the volume went way up." (S012, 2003-10-03)
- "we could raise prices 10% a year and no one cared." (S024, 2011-07-02)
- "Get it low and keep it there forever." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- **Read:** See's and the Costco hot dog are two different jobs. One branded pleasure can take a price increase every year. The price that stands for the whole promise cannot move (inferred from S003, S024, S038).
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S012, S024, S030, S038. On a building, Wesco charged just under the market and picked tenants it admired (S002).

## What you pay
- **Stance:** A wonderful business is still a bad buy at a high enough price. Pay a fair price for a good business you understand. In a crazy boom, do not pay a high multiple.
- "No business is so wonderful that you can’t overpay for it." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "there's no investment that is so good you can't ruin it by raising the price higher and higher" (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "I didn't get rich by buying stocks at a high price earnings multiples in the midst of crazy speculative booms, and I'm not going to change." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- **Sources:** S004, S007, S024, S026, S027, S036, S038, S039. The 1996 line and the 2023 line match. The 2015 Berkshire letter adds the buying rule he used: a fair price for a good business the chairman understands, with a good chief executive in place (S027). Dexter Shoe is the case where paying up, and paying in shares, hurt (S024, S039).

## Debt, leverage, and liquidity
- **Stance:** Keep the ability to act in a bad year. Little debt, real liquidity, and no lender who can force a sale at the bottom. The amount of debt still depends on the case. He has no single ratio.
- **Over time:**
  - 1987-02-13: borrow some long-term money before you need it, so hazards and chances can both be met. "to do a certain amount of long-term borrowing in advance of specific need, in order to have maximum financial flexibility to face both hazards and opportunities" (S002)
  - 2015-02-28, **newer emphasis:** "Berkshire would have little debt outstanding as it tried to maintain (i) virtually perfect creditworthiness" (S027)
  - 2015-04-07, **newer:** "The appropriate amount of debt varies with the circumstances. I don't have any general rule." (S028)
- "Why would you risk what you have and need in order to get what you don’t have and don’t need?" (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "Human nature needs margin requirements, just as it needs restrictions on selling heroin to 14-year-olds." (S018, 2008-01-01)
- "We strive to maintain much liquidity to provide a margin of safety against short-term equity price volatility." (S021, 2009-02-25)
- "conduct your life so that you can handle the 50% decline with aplomb and grace." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- **Sources:** S002, S018, S021, S022, S027, S028, S030, S032, S033. He expects democracies to borrow too much eventually and says he does not know when (S033).
- **Read:** the 1987 borrow-ahead line is about flexibility. The 2015 lines reject leverage as a way to look busy or to please activists (inferred from S002, S027, S028).

## Fees and outside managers
- **Stance:** A layer of fees, taken every year, eats the result. Most outside managers will not beat a plain index after fees. Charge extra only for results that are actually unusual. Prefer the manager who does not scrape a fee when the client is down.
- "As long as clients buy salt, investment managers will sell it." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "And of course the high fees will just kill you" (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "you’re not losing 40 percent of your future you’re losing 90 percent." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "most of the people who are in wealth management have an almost zero chance of outperforming an unmanaged index like the S&P." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "you really shouldn't be in the business of charging extra unless you really are going to achieve very unusual results." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- "It’s what I call fee-driven buying, much of it, where people are buying things to get the fees." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- **Sources:** S003, S008, S028, S030, S032, S033, S034, S038, S039. He calls a 3 percent annual waste on a foundation real and probably too low a figure (S008). He prefers to beat the average himself and does not use the index for that job (S024).

## Raising money and venture capital
- **Stance:** Easy money causes bad behavior. Returns get harder as the pile of capital grows. Modern venture capital, in his late view, is fee-driven and dressed-up investment banking. A venture bet is allowed only with open eyes, at a size you can lose.
- "Large amounts of easy money cause regrettable human behavior. That’s Munger’s rule." (S029, 2016-02-11)
- "easy money will always attract wretched excess." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- "prospects for above-average returns tend to go down as assets managed go up" (S002, 1987-02-13)
- "I regard modern venture capital as investment banking in disguise." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "You're all just sort of gambling." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- "In our judgment, the prospect for gain justified the risk of loss." (S002, 1987-02-13)
- **Sources:** S002, S028, S029, S034, S038, S039. The 2014 and 2015 Daily Journal meetings call the company's own software push a venture bet he hates (S026, S028). Sequoia is the exception he names, because it stayed small (S034). He would hate to compete with Sequoia on its own ground (S036).
- **No recorded position** on priced rounds, valuation caps, or how much a startup should raise. Nearest: do not take money that makes you feel you must act (S026), and do not sell a piece of a company to a buyer you do not trust (S039).

## Crypto
- **Stance:** He treats crypto as a gambling contract with the house edge, not as money, and he wants it banned. The view gets harder over time. It does not flip.
- **Over time:**
  - 2017-12-20: "I think it's perfectly asinine to even pause to think about them." (S031)
  - 2018-02-14: "Bitcoin is noxious poison." (S032)
  - 2020-02-12: "I don’t know much about crypto technologies except to avoid them." (S034)
  - 2021-02-24: "And since I never buy any gold, I never buy any Bitcoin." (S036)
  - 2023-02-01, **newer:** "A cryptocurrency is not a currency, not a commodity, and not a security." (S037) "a gambling contract with a nearly 100% edge for the house" (S037) "Obviously the U.S. should now enact a new federal law that prevents this from happening." (S037)
  - 2023-12-05, **newer:** "You can call it a store of wealth, I call it a store of delusion." (S039) "I think cryptocurrency ought to have been driven out as illegal." (S039)
- **Sources:** S031, S032, S034, S036, S037, S039. He likes gold better than crypto and still does not buy gold (S036, S039).

## Buying versus building
- **Stance:** Buy a business that already works, with the operators still in place, at a fair price. Shareholders usually lose when managers acquire. Starting from a cigar butt, or from zero, made less money for him than buying in.
- "we made way more money by finding something that's already working in a business and just buying in" (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "We generally buy with the talent in place." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "Study after study shows shareholders lose when management acquires businesses." (S024, 2011-07-02)
- "You can't fix these really lousy businesses." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- **Sources:** S002, S007, S024, S027, S031, S038, S039. He wants no acquisitions department under pressure to buy, and no advisor who is paid if the deal happens (S027). Crowded buyers push prices to modest returns for a low-leverage buyer (S002).
- **Limit:** He still made a few venture-style bets (the Bowery, BYD, Daily Journal software) and he labels them as such (S002, S028, S029).

## A new product inside the company
- **Stance:** A second business with long odds is a venture bet. He dislikes that bet and still made it when the niche was hard, slow, and sticky. Spend the money if you are rich and the spend is what lets you compete. Expect it to be worse before it is better.
- "It was really, as I've said again and again, a venture capital-type activity, which I hate and isn't why I made my way in life." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "It’s venture capital. It just happens to be located in a publicly traded company." (S029, 2016-02-11)
- "I kind of like it if it's so hard, because if you win it'll be pretty hard to take away." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "There's no point in being rich if you don't use it to compete effectively." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "Anything that's really likely to work, is likely to be hard." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- **Sources:** S010, S013, S026, S028, S029, S030. A startup inside CORT lost money and was later folded in (S010, S013, S014). He says the Daily Journal software decision may not have been right at the time (S028).

## What you sell
- **Stance:** Sell what you would buy yourself, and what is good for the user. The high road is less crowded. Do not profit by tricking a customer, and do not use a cost-plus contract that pays you to be slow.
- "You should try to make money by selling people things that are good for the customer." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "You should only sell products that are good for the people who use them." (S024, 2011-07-02)
- "sell what you would buy if you were the buyer, not what you can sell by misleading people." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "I take the high road because it’s less crowded." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- "We don’t get the right to collect money until the thing works and we do that on purpose." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- **Sources:** S003, S024, S026, S030, S034, S036, S039. "I don’t think capitalism requires that you make all the money that you can." (S030, 2017-02-15)

## Partners
- **Stance:** Pick partners you like, trust, and would want to work under. A short talk with a trusted person beats a long contract with someone you do not trust. The partner who puts up the money and takes the loss is rare and should be kept. Being the only deep pocket is a bad business.
- "I’ve had marvelous partners all my life." (S017, 2007-05-13)
- "it helps if you like one another and enjoy working together." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- "I'd rather have a brief telephone with somebody I trust than I would a 40-page contract prepared by the finest law firm in the world with somebody I don't trust." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "That behavior is so rare, so noble, you shouldn't squelch it." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "being the only deep pocket around is bad business" (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "The difference between the number one and number two is like going off a cliff." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- **Sources:** S004, S017, S026, S030, S036, S038, S039. He and Buffett do not debate so much as adapt (S039). A partnership, he says, was the right Berkshire structure, and the corporate form was an accident (S026).

## Hiring and firing
- **Stance:** The paper record predicts better than an interview. Buy the talent with the business. Hire for the ability to learn and for someone who knows his limits. Search firms will sell you the best available even when that person is poor. Remove the person who should have gone, and do it sooner.
- "The paper record has about three times the predictive value of your impression in an interview." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "We generally buy with the talent in place." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "I almost despise the business of executive search because I find that they really want to sell you the best that's available even if there's no damn good." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "Of course I want the guy who understands his limitations instead of the guy who doesn’t." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "It won't matter. He'll be able to do it." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- "somebody who should have been removed wasn’t for way too long." (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "Maybe that’s the reason we hire so few of them." (S012, 2003-10-03)
- **Sources:** S012, S027, S031, S032, S033, S036. Choose the CEO for trustworthiness, skill, energy, and love of the business, and do not force retirement for age alone (S027). One recruiter find, Ajit Jain, is the exception he names to his dislike of search (S031).
- **No recorded position** on when a startup makes its first hire.

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Almost never sell a decent business. Find the thing you do not need to exit. When a sale does happen, sell to people you like and admire.
- "Berkshire would almost never sell a subsidiary." (S027, 2015-02-28)
- "But if it's a halfway decent business, we never sell anything." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- "I’m trying never to have to exit." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "So I say find Costco’s, not good exits." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "Generally speaking, we like selling to people we like and admire." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- **Sources:** S026, S027, S033, S036, S038. He says he is too loyal when selling a winner, and he has no system for a nosebleed price (S036).
- **Read:** hold is the rule from 2015 through 2023. The 2014 line is about the buyer, on the rare sale (inferred from S026, S027, S038).

## Brand
- **Stance:** The business is the habit and the name people ask for. Share of mind matters more than a raw share number. Make the product better before you put marketing behind it. Big household brands, in the later view, have a harder century ahead.
- "mere association has enormous power" (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "So it is not share of market but share of mind that counts." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "First, you make the product truly superior. Then, and only then, will we put our marketing muscle behind it." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "We will never get very rich from this business except by using a trademark and creating a brand strong enough that people order our new beverage by its trademarked name." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- 2023-12-05, **newer:** "I just think that there's more trouble coming to big brands than they've had in the last 100 years." (S039)
- **Read:** the 1996 lesson is how a loved name gets built. The 2023 line is house brands pressing on the big names. He does not take back See's (inferred from S004, S024, S039).
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S024, S032, S038, S039.

## Culture and bureaucracy
- **Stance:** Trust is an economic force. Decentralize, keep headquarters nearly empty, and treat bureaucracy as something that grows in success and then breeds failure. He does not claim a cure for a big bureaucratic place.
- "Trust is one of the greatest economic forces on earth." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "We’ve decentralized all the power except for natural headquarters-type capital allocation." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "The constant curse of scale is that it leads to big, dumb bureaucracy" (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "What big businesses have in common by and large is that they get very bureaucratic." (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "Modern life creates successful bureaucracy and successful bureaucracy breeds failure and stupidity." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "I don’t know how to fix bureaucracy in a big place." (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "They can't be bureaucratic if they're not there." (S039, 2023-12-05)
- "delegation just short of abdication" (S018, 2008-01-01)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S012, S017, S018, S027, S032, S033, S036, S038, S039. Culture plus the model is how he explains Costco (S038). He never owned General Electric because he disliked the culture (S036).

## Niche
- **Stance:** A narrow product that a small group must have can beat a broad one. The good niche is the one big competitors find painful, so they stay out, and the customer, once won, stays.
- "A magazine called Motorcross is a total necessity to those people." (S003, 1994-04-14)
- "The really big boys find our niche in the software market such absolute agony that they tend to stay out of it." (S029, 2016-02-11)
- "once you succeed, it’s very sticky business." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- "God bless our stupid competitors. They make us rich." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- **Sources:** S003, S029, S030, S034. Government court software is slow and political, which is why rivals avoid it (S034).

## Changing your mind
- **Stance:** Destroy a favorite idea on purpose. Change the conclusion when the facts change. He and Buffett treat that as a skill, and he names places they did it.
- "However, both Warren and I are very good at changing our prior conclusions." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "I think it’s my duty to destroy old ideas." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- "Certainly it’s ok to change your mind when the facts change." (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "I actually work at trying to discard beliefs." (S034, 2020-02-12)
- **Sources:** S004, S030, S032, S034, S039. Recorded changes: Apple, airlines, and railroads after the facts moved (S030, S032); more discomfort on China by 2023 (S039); a revised view of Jack Welch's methods (S039).
- **China, over time:**
  - 2019-02-14: "The great companies of China are cheaper than the great companies of the United States." (S033)
  - 2023-12-05, **newer:** "Obviously, I'm more uncomfortable now than I was." (S039)

## Banks and credit
- **Stance:** Banking is a good business only when the people in it are trying not to lose money. Optimism, easy standards, and reported earnings are the failure modes. If the street is making dumb loans, shrink and wait.
- "And a banker should not be an optimist." (S031, 2017-12-20)
- "All intelligent investors worry about banks because banks present temptations to their managers to do dumb things." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "I like banking when they're trying to avoid losses prudently." (S035, 2020-12-16)
- "the temptations to do something stupid are way greater in banking than they are in most businesses." (S032, 2018-02-14)
- "you can’t really trust the figures put out by the banking industry." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- "Bad morals drive out the good." (S022, 2010-01-19)
- **Sources:** S022, S024, S029, S030, S031, S032, S033, S035, S036. Loosening standards because everyone else did is his account of the last crisis (S026). He picks a few careful banks and says a friend who avoids all banks is understandable (S024).

## Marketing and paid ads
- **Stance:** Marketing follows a product that is actually better. A known name and heavy advertising are part of building a habit, as in the Coke exercise. Ubiquity is part of the product.
- "First, you make the product truly superior. Then, and only then, will we put our marketing muscle behind it." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "we know a heavy percentage of revenues will have to go into advertising" (S004, 1996-04-19)
- "So availability has to be a religion in our new business." (S004, 1996-04-19)
- **No recorded position** on buying users with paid ads, or on a startup's ad budget. Nearest: do not spend the marketing muscle on a product that is not truly better (S004), and do not raise price so you can bribe the buyer's agent (S012, S018).

## Spending
- **Stance:** Spend less than you earn, then invest the gap. Use the surplus when it is the thing that lets you compete. Do not take the last dollar from a customer in distress.
- "We always underspent our incomes and invested." (S033, 2019-02-14)
- "If Mozart can’t get by with this kind of asinine conduct, I don’t think you should try it." (S017, 2007-05-13)
- "There's no point in being rich if you don't use it to compete effectively." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "I don’t think capitalism requires that you make all the money that you can." (S030, 2017-02-15)
- "a penny saved is a penny earned," (S021, 2009-02-25)
- **Sources:** S017, S018, S021, S028, S030, S033, S035. He calls deferred gratification a long-run advantage (S034). In the foreclosure boom, Daily Journal had the share to raise prices a lot and did not (S030, S033).

## Gambling, derivatives, and promotions
- **Stance:** Be the house if you must be in a game of odds. Do not build a business that lures people into stock gambling, and do not treat derivatives as innovation. Wall Street will sell what can be sold.
- "You want to be the house, not the punter." (S038, 2023-10-29)
- "I don’t like the vast growth of legalized gambling, including the legalized gambling run by Wall Street in the form of derivatives trading and so on." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "It's OK to be boring in finance. What we want is innovation in widgets." (S024, 2011-07-02)
- "it's really stupid to have a culture which encourages as much gambling on stocks" (S036, 2021-02-24)
- "the investment banking profession will sell shit as long as shit can be sold." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- "Commission-free trading is not free." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- **Sources:** S012, S018, S020, S022, S024, S026, S036, S037, S038. He would not own assets that exist only until someone reaches for them (S018). Sports betting, in 2023, is not good for the country in his view (S038).

## Work and temperament
- **Stance:** A long attention span beats a display of brightness. Multitasking costs the best thought. Reliability outranks other virtues. Low expectations make the work bearable. Interest in the subject is a condition of doing it well.
- "I did not succeed in life by intelligence. I succeeded because I have a long attention span." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "I think people who multitask pay a huge price." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- "If you’re unreliable it doesn’t matter what your virtues are, you’re going to crater" (S017, 2007-05-13)
- "The secret of human felicity is not vast ambition, it's low expectations." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "I have never succeeded very much in anything in which I was not very interested." (S026, 2014-09-19)
- "Other people are trying to be smart; all I'm trying to be is non-idiotic." (S028, 2015-04-07)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S017, S026, S028, S029, S034, S036. He tells people to deserve what they want (S017, S020). He says determined rationality works, and that most people should specialize rather than copy his spread of interests (S029, S030, S034).

## AI
- **Stance:** As of 2018 he does not study artificial intelligence, and he does not assume it is an economic revolution. His substitute is organized common sense.
- "I’ve done so well in life by just using organized common sense, that I never wanted to get into these fields like artificial intelligence." (S032, 2018-02-14)
- **Sources:** S032. The 2017 meeting notes record him as agnostic on whether a computer system can predict payments (S030). **No later recorded position** through the 2023 interviews. Nearest later line: complicated technology stays outside his circle (S034, S039).

## Remote work and offices
- **Stance:** By early 2021 he expects less travel, more remote meetings, and trouble for office buildings. That is the whole recorded view.
- "I think we're going to do a lot less travel and a lot more Zooming." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- "I think office buildings are now in some trouble." (S036, 2021-02-24)
- **No recorded position** on whether an early team should be remote, or on how to run a distributed company.

## Discounting
- **No recorded position** on discounts as a sales tactic. Nearest: do not raise prices on customers in distress (S030, 2017-02-15; S033, 2019-02-14), and do not raise the price of the item that carries the promise. "Get it low and keep it there forever." (S038, 2023-10-29)

## Content
- **No recorded position** on content as a way to get customers. He treats heavy reading as part of how a person stays rational (S017, S028, S029). That is a personal practice, not a channel plan.

## Founder equity splits
- **No recorded position** on how founders should divide shares, vesting, or a 50/50 split. Nearest: trust beats a long contract (S039, 2023-12-05), and an owner who already has control should think hard before also taking the salary and the fees. "maybe you shouldn’t try and grab all the money in addition." (S030, 2017-02-15)

## Burnout
- **No recorded position** on burnout, time off, or a ceiling on hours. Nearest: low expectations (S026, S036), a long attention span rather than a sprint of cleverness (S028), and one line for hard times. "I have one standard set of advice for all difficulties, suck it in and cope." (S039, 2023-12-05)
