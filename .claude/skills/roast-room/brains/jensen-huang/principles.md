# Principles: Jensen Huang

15 decision rules, built from the NVIDIA GTC, COMPUTEX, and SIGGRAPH keynotes, 2021 to 2026. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Carry the stack that sets the speed, or take that layer from a platform that already has it.** An application waiting on a general-purpose computer is waiting on a curve he says has stopped. Inferred from S001, S004, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014. "Accelerated computing is not a chip problem." (S013) "Blackwell is not a chip. Blackwell is the name of a platform." (S007) Sources: 10 (S001, S004, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

2. **Build far enough down the stack to understand the system, then open every layer.** Partners should be able to take the whole platform or one piece, and the customer's data stays the customer's. Inferred from S001, S002, S007, S008, S011, S012, S013, S014. "we try to build as much of it as we can so that we understand it but then we open it up so everybody can access it." (S007) "NVIDIA is a vertically integrated computing company with open horizontal integration with the world." (S013) Sources: 8 (S001, S002, S007, S008, S011, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

3. **Keep the architecture compatible, and make the installed base faster after the sale.** New hardware that strands old software fails the platform test. The desk machine and the data center machine should be the same architecture. Inferred from S001, S002, S006, S007, S008, S009, S011, S012, S013, S014. "Software inertia is the single most important thing in computers." (S008) "Years after purchase, our chips keep getting faster and better." (S001) Sources: 10 (S001, S002, S006, S007, S008, S009, S011, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

4. **Judge the machine on tokens per watt and cost per token, inside the power and the building you have to pay for anyway.** A cheaper chip, or a free one, loses when the architecture wastes that fixed power. Inferred from S004, S006, S007, S008, S010, S012, S013, S014. "if you have the wrong architecture, even if it's free, it's not cheap enough." (S013) "Performance per watt is your revenues." (S014) Sources: 8 (S004, S006, S007, S008, S010, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

5. **Plot factory throughput against per-user speed, and pick the design that grows the area under the curve.** The two axes compete, so one box that claims both extremes is the wrong promise. Tier the service. Inferred from S007, S010, S011, S013, S014. "your goal is to maximize the area under the curve" (S010) "optimizing for high throughput and optimizing for low latency are, in fact, enemies of each other." (S013) Sources: 5 (S007, S010, S011, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

6. **Rehearse on a digital twin before a large physical deployment, including the data center.** Volume products and giant factories do not get their first real test in production. Inferred from S001, S002, S003, S004, S007, S010, S011, S012, S014. "No physical product operated by software can be deployed in large numbers without testing the software on its digital twin." (S003) "before you build a data center you have to build a digital twin" (S010) Sources: 9 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S007, S010, S011, S012, S014). Confidence: high.

7. **The builder rewrites the software. A straight port onto new hardware can run slower.** Where the rewrite can be hidden in a library, the customer's adoption should feel like no code change. Inferred from S001, S004, S008, S009, S012. "You in fact have to rewrite the software. That's the hard part." (S008) "if you just did that, it actually runs slower." (S012) Sources: 5 (S001, S004, S008, S009, S012). Confidence: high.

8. **Ship physical AI on three computers: train, simulate, and run.** In September 2022 he counted two, a factory to develop the robot and a computer in the machine. From March 2024 the simulation computer is the third. Inferred from S001, S002, S003, S007, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014. "Robotics requires two computer systems: one being the AI factory for developing the robot AI, and the other an AI computer to operate the robot." (S003) "Physical AI requires three computers." (S012) Sources: 10 (S001, S002, S003, S007, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

9. **One chip will not deliver the next generation. Co-design the system, the network, the library, and the model together.** Keep the programming model stable while those layers move. Inferred from S001, S004, S011, S012, S013, S014. "You're not going to take one chip and make a computer 10 times faster." (S012) "You can't just design chips and hope that things on top of it is gonna go faster." (S012) Sources: 6 (S001, S004, S011, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

10. **Publish the roadmap years ahead, change one hard layer at a time, and revise a name when the stack under it leaps.** Buyers are planning land, power, and capital on what you show them. Inferred from S003, S008, S010, S012, S013. "which is the reason why I show you our road map a couple two three years in advance" (S010) "we try to take silicon risk or networking risk or system chassis risk um in in pieces so that we can move the industry forward" (S010) Sources: 5 (S003, S008, S010, S012, S013). Confidence: high.

11. **Treat power as the cap on revenue. Accelerate every workload you can, and keep the expensive machines full.** Saved watts go back into compute. Idle time on a power-limited factory is lost revenue. Inferred from S003, S004, S006, S007, S008, S010, S011, S013, S014. "First and foremost, data centers must accelerate every workload." (S004) "every single data center in the future will be power limited your revenues are power limited" (S010) Sources: 9 (S003, S004, S006, S007, S008, S010, S011, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

12. **Scale up into one coherent system before you scale out across a network.** Adding ordinary nodes is the easy direction. The hard computer is the one that behaves as a single machine. Use copper as far as it reaches. Inferred from S007, S010, S011, S012. "you want to scale up first before you scale out" (S010) "Scaling up is incredibly hard." (S011) Sources: 4 (S007, S010, S011, S012). Confidence: high.

13. **Build the developer install base before the applications arrive, and say yes when a developer asks for the machine.** Libraries are useful because that base already exists. Inferred from S001, S003, S004, S007, S008, S010, S011, S012, S013. "you need an install base 1st" (S008) "If a developer reaches out to you, need a GPU, the answer is yes." (S011) Sources: 9 (S001, S003, S004, S007, S008, S010, S011, S012, S013). Confidence: high.

14. **Once tokens are the product, more committed compute is more revenue for the buyer.** From 2021 through 2024 his line to buyers is that buying more lowers the cost of the work. From 2025 the line is that buying more raises revenue, because the computer sells tokens. Inferred from S001, S003, S006, S008, S010, S014. "The more you buy, the more you save." (S001) "The more you buy, the more you make." (S014) Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S006, S008, S010, S014). Confidence: high.

15. **Build the reference system yourself and run it, then let partners copy any layer.** The blueprint is a machine you operate, not a slide. Inferred from S002, S008, S014. "Eos will be the blueprint for the most advanced AI infrastructure for our OEM and cloud partners." (S002) "we need a reference platform for robotic systems." (S014) Sources: 3 (S002, S008, S014). Confidence: high.
