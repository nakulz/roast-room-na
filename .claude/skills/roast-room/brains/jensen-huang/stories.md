# Stories: Jensen Huang

13 stories he tells in these sources, with the lesson and source ids. Dates on the quotes are the source dates.

1. **CUDA, the overnight success that took 20 years.** He launched CUDA in 2006 expecting it to hit at once, and says it took almost 20 years (S007). In 2026 he says they could not afford it, that it consumed the vast majority of the company's profits, and that they held it for 13 generations, about 20 years, carried on GeForce (S013). "For 20 years, we've been dedicated to this architecture" (S013). Lesson: keep paying for the platform bet after the quick win fails to show (inferred from S007, S013).
2. **RTX was a bet-the-company moment.** At SIGGRAPH 2018 he put ray tracing and AI into one GPU because rasterization was reaching its limit. Five years later he says the room did not know it was a bet-the-company moment, and that the bet paid off. The 2018 demo had 2.5 million polygons. Racer RTX has 250 million (S006). "You didn't know it at the time, but we did, that it was a 'bet the company' moment." (S006) "The bet has paid off." (S006). Lesson: take the architecture risk when the old method is maxed out, and score it years later.
3. **The first DGX, and nobody wanted it.** He was certain nobody wanted to buy it, announced it, and OpenAI, then a small San Francisco company, asked him to deliver one (S008, S004, S007, S012). He also tells that launch as confusion, no customers and no applause, after billions of dollars, then says he does not know the life lesson and offers another: if a developer asks for a GPU, say yes (S011). "in fact I was certain nobody wanted to buy it" (S008) "I don't know what the life lesson is there." (S011) Lesson: ship the reference machine before the market can explain it (inferred from S004, S007, S008, S011, S012).
4. **Taiwan and the partner ecosystem.** He calls Taiwan the home of his partners and the world's best supply chain ecosystem, and he opens with a childhood night market, a scar, and a fruit seller of 43 years (S008, S014). Vera Rubin draws on about 150 supply chain partners there (S014). Making VMware CUDA-aware took about two and a half years (S006). "Taiwan is the home of our treasured partners." (S008) "the world's best supply chain ecosystem." (S014). Lesson: the full stack ships because the upstream ecosystem can build it, and wiring a partner in takes years (inferred from S006, S008, S014).
5. **Digital twins, from Apollo 13 to the plant.** He tells Apollo 13 as the origin: NASA fixed the accident on a replica on Earth and later coined the digital twin (S002). He then uses the industrial cases: a Siemens Energy steam-generator twin that could cut downtime 70% and save the industry about $1.7 billion a year, BMW expanding from one factory twin to four and building an EV plant in simulation two years before it opens, Wistron cutting build time in half, and Foxconn's Houston plant born digital before construction (S001, S004, S007, S012). Lesson: simulate the expensive thing first, and one working site becomes the rollout (inferred from S001, S002, S004, S007, S012).
6. **Atlan is no more.** In September 2022 he drops Atlan, the robotics chip he had named the year before, and replaces it with Thor because Grace, Hopper, and Ada made the next processor too good to leave out (S003). "Today, we are announcing that Atlan is no more..." (S003) "There are three big reasons we did that: Grace, Hopper, and Ada Lovelace." (S003). Lesson: kill a public roadmap when the stack gets better.
7. **AlexNet, then the whole company.** The team stepped back and asked what they were witnessing and how far it would scale, and he says that look is what turned the company toward deep learning (S009). The moment he saw AlexNet, he decided to go all in on self-driving cars (S010). He also retells it as a cat going in and the word cat coming out, a new way to write software (S007). "We had the benefit of taking a step back and asking ourselves, what are we witnessing?" (S009) "the moment I saw Alexnet was such an inspiring moment" (S010). Lesson: when a result generalizes, stop and aim the company at what scales (inferred from S007, S009, S010).
8. **Ranger was too big, and the GPU name was wrong.** Three years before the 2025 keynote he showed Ranger, an early NVLink 32 machine. It was way too large, and he says it was exactly the right idea, so they re-engineered scale-up into a form that could ship (S010). In the same talk he says calling a two-die Blackwell package one GPU was wrong, because it broke the link count. "it was way too large but it was exactly the right idea" (S010) "we call that one chip a GPU and that was wrong" (S010). Lesson: prove the idea in a crude form, then fix the shape and correct yourself in public.
9. **cuLitho took nearly seven years.** He says the lithography library took nearly seven years before TSMC, Samsung, and ASML used it, and he files it with the other domain libraries that opened a market one at a time (S012). Lesson: a new industry on the platform is a multi-year library bet, not a chip announcement.
10. **Twelve GPUs against two thousand CPUs.** The Google Brain result he cites needed 2,000 CPUs. Twelve NVIDIA GPUs matched that deep-learning performance (S015). "As it turned out, 12 NVIDIA GPUs could deliver the deep-learning performance of 2,000 CPUs." (S015) Lesson: the new software model needed a new computer, and a small accelerator cluster beat a giant CPU room.
11. **The Mellanox breakfast.** The bid started at breakfast in his hotel room at SC18, on 18 November 2018. He told Eyal that NVIDIA would be the final and highest bidder (S017). "Our journey started in my hotel room at SC18 on November 18, 2018." (S017) "I told him that NVIDIA would be the final and highest bidder." (S017) Lesson: when faster compute makes moving data the bottleneck, buy the network.
12. **Project Grace.** In April 2021, PCIe was the bottleneck and no x86 CPU had NVLink. He announced NVIDIA's first data center CPU, named for Grace Hopper, Arm-based and built for accelerated computing (S020). "Today, we're announcing our first data center CPU, Project Grace, named after Grace Hopper, a computer scientist and U.S. Navy rear Admiral, who in the '50s pioneered computer programming." (S020) Lesson: design the CPU for the accelerator when the old socket is the limit.
13. **Earth-2.** In November 2021 he says simulation is how you see a future in time to act on it (S021). "To make our future a reality today, simulation is the answer." (S021) Lesson: a problem you cannot rerun in the world gets a twin first.
