# Positions: Andrew Ng

40 topics from the notes on 16 sources (S001 to S016, 2007 to 2022, S016 undated). Most-cited first, with ties broken toward the decision a founder would bring this seat. No evidence means no recorded position. The newer view is marked where he moved.

## More data
Sources: S001, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S016.
More data is a variance tool. Buy it when the model already fits the examples it has seen and still misses new ones. Leave it when training error is already worse than the target you need.
- Undated, and the notes place these pages in Winter 2003: on a problem the data separates with a margin, the perceptron's mistakes do not grow just because the sequence gets longer. "does not have an explicit dependence on the number of examples m in the sequence, or on the dimension n of the inputs (!)." (S016, undated).
- 2012-11-26: volume cannot rescue a model of the wrong kind. "even if we were fitting a linear model to a very large amount of training data, the linear model would still fail to accurately capture the structure in the data." (S005, 2012-11-26) Holding out a large slice is a fair price only when data is cheap. "While this is fine if data is abundant and/or cheap, in learning problems in which data is scarce" (S004, 2012-11-26) With enough data, a locally weighted fit makes the exact feature choice less critical. "makes the choice of features less critical." (S006, 2012-09-29).
- 2015-10-12: a small set favors the model with stronger assumptions, because it needs less data. "GDA makes stronger modeling assumptions, and is more data efficient" (S007, 2015-10-12) On a very large non-Gaussian set, logistic regression almost always beats the stronger Gaussian model (S007).
- 2018: high variance means add data. High training error means more data will not reach the target, and adding examples can make the training fit worse. Plot the learning curve before a months-long collection. In theory, enough data can drive variance near zero. "If you have high variance, add data to your training set." (S001, 2018) "It could be a waste of time to work on getting more data." (S001, 2018) "Now, you can be absolutely sure that adding more data will not, by itself, be sufficient." (S001, 2018) "there’s no way you’re getting 95% accuracy on examples your algorithm hasn’t even seen." (S001, 2018) "Looking at the learning curve might therefore help you avoid spending months collecting twice as much training data, only to realize it does not help." (S001, 2018) "In theory, we can always reduce variance to nearly zero by training on a massive training set." (S001, 2018) "adding more examples to your training set only makes it harder for your algorithm to do well on the training set." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20. Newer. On many problems, bigger sets plus neural nets, and no new algorithm, go further. He still sees teams spend six months collecting because they heard that data is valuable. "And bigger data sets with neural networks, without further algorithm innovation, will be sufficient to take us further." (S003, 2020-02-20) "you could run some tests and could have figured out six months earlier that for this particular problem, collecting more data isn't going to cut it." (S003, 2020-02-20).
Name the error before you fund a collection (inferred from S001, S003, S005). Use the 2020 bet when you are far from the ceiling and the curve still slopes. Keep the 2012 warning: data does not repair a model that cannot represent the pattern.

## Which model to try
Sources: S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S007, S008.
Pick the family from the kind of target and from how sure you are about the shape of the data. Judge the choice on data the model was not fit on. Core ideas outrank the name of the model.
- 2012-09-29: match the family to the target (a number, a yes or no, a count, many classes). More features are not automatically better. "Naively, it might seem that the more features we add, the better." (S006, 2012-09-29).
- 2012-11-26: choosing by training error always crowns the most complex model. "This algorithm does not work." (S004, 2012-11-26) The same held-out procedure chooses among very different families, not only among polynomial degrees (S004).
- 2015-10-12: use the stronger assumptions when you trust the shape of the data. Use the weaker ones when you do not. A crude method that is easy to build is worth running first. "Logistic regression makes weaker assumptions, and is significantly more robust to deviations from modeling assumptions." (S007, 2015-10-12) "the Naive Bayes classifier often works surprisingly well." (S007, 2015-10-12) "even though the Naive Bayes assumption is an extremely strong assumptions, the resulting algorithm works well on many problems." (S007, 2015-10-12).
- 2015-10-20: he places support vector machines among the best off-the-shelf supervised learners, and says many people believe they are the best. A plain polynomial or Gaussian kernel got extremely good digit results with no vision knowledge built in (S008). "SVMs are among the best (and many believe are indeed the best)" (S008, 2015-10-20).
- 2018: with about 20 examples, hand-built features move the result more than the choice of algorithm. At 1 million examples he favors the neural network (S001). "the hand-engineering of features will have a bigger effect than the choice of algorithm." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20. Newer. Keep a portfolio. Deep learning is one tool in it. Around 2005 a napkin argument from Geoffrey Hinton convinced him unsupervised learning would matter, and early Google Brain bet on it. In this interview he calls that early bet the mistake, and calls scale the part they got right. Self-supervised learning is a way to manufacture labels and only a piece of the answer. Reinforcement learning was not what was working in deployment, and he said the field moves fast enough that this could change. "but the AI team shouldn't just use deep learning." (S003, 2020-02-20) "some days we use a neural net, some days we use a PCA." (S003, 2020-02-20) "The thing we really got wrong was the importance of, the early importance of unsupervised learning." (S003, 2020-02-20) "The part we got right was the importance of scale." (S003, 2020-02-20) "I don't think there's a whole enchilada. I think it's just a piece of it." (S003, 2020-02-20) "And I think this is a way to generate infinite label data." (S003, 2020-02-20) "It's not what's working today." (S003, 2020-02-20) "Now our world changes so fast, maybe it'll be totally different in a couple of years." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13. Newer on depth of math. As methods become turnkey, less mathematics is required to make them run. The concepts, including bias, variance, and error analysis, still come before any one architecture. He also dropped an old habit of shipping the first idea that excited him. "Beyond specific models, it’s even more important to understand the core concepts behind how and why machine learning works" (S002, 2022-12-13) "As machine learning techniques mature and become more reliable and turnkey, they require less debugging, and a shallower understanding of the math involved may be sufficient to make them work." (S002, 2022-12-13) "When I was younger, I used to execute on the first idea I was excited about." (S002, 2022-12-13).
Use the 2020 portfolio and the 2018 error split. Treat the 2015 ranking of support vector machines as the older off-the-shelf claim.

## Small data, labels, and features
Sources: S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S011.
Outside consumer internet, the set is small. Then the model has to be restricted, the features have to be chosen, and the labels have to be clean. A big internet company can average noisy labels. A hundred-example inspection line cannot.
- 2012-11-26: when examples are not comfortably above the number of dimensions, a full covariance is a poor fit, and even one Gaussian is hard. Restrict the model. "unless m exceeds n by some reasonable amount" (S011, 2012-11-26) "difficult to model the data even with a single Gaussian, much less a mixture of Gaussian." (S011, 2012-11-26).
- 2015-10-12: stronger assumptions buy data efficiency (see Which model to try). A count of zero in a finite sample is not a probability of zero. "it is statistically a bad idea to estimate the probability of some event to be zero" (S007, 2015-10-12).
- 2018: on a small training set, feature selection matters, and most of what the system knows has to come from people. Tolerate some bad dev and test labels at the start. Fix them once they are a large share of the remaining error, and fix dev and test by the same process. Teams often change their mind and clean labels only after the system improves. "But when your training set is small, feature selection can be very useful." (S001, 2018) "most of your algorithm’s knowledge will have to come from your human insight." (S001, 2018) "If the fraction of the dev set that is mislabeled impedes your ability to make these judgments, then it is worth spending time to fix the mislabeled dev set labels." (S001, 2018) "It is not uncommon to start off tolerating some mislabeled dev/test set examples, only later to change your mind as your system improves" (S001, 2018) "30% of your errors are due to the mislabeled dev set images, adding significant error to your estimates of accuracy." (S001, 2018) "Whatever process you apply to fixing dev set labels, remember to apply it to the test set labels too" (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20, split by setting. The 2018 label rule still stands. "The data sets are really small, so we face small data problems." (S003, 2020-02-20) "If you have only a hundred examples, these little labeling errors, if 10 of your hundred labels are wrong, that actually is 10% of your data set has a big impact." (S003, 2020-02-20) "In the case of large consumer internet companies where you have a billion users, you have a lot of data, you don't worry about it, just take the average, it kind of works." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: the job, in practice, is iterative cleaning. "learn how much time most machine learning engineers spend iteratively cleaning datasets." (S002, 2022-12-13).
Average labels only in the billion-user case. Everywhere else, write the labeling rule down and clean (inferred from S001, S003).

## Bias, variance, and model size
Sources: S001, S003, S004, S005, S012.
High error has two different causes, and they ask for different work. Bias is the error you would still have with endless data of this kind. Variance is fitting accidents of a finite sample. He wants the unavoidable floor, usually a human number, subtracted before anyone picks a lever.
- 2012-11-26: he calls bias and variance the twin evils, and says the right model size sits between a line that underfits and a curve that memorizes. Cutting input dimension shrinks the hypothesis class. The two failures can look alike. "twin evils of bias and variance" (S004, 2012-11-26) "Often, there is a tradeoff between bias and variance." (S005, 2012-11-26) "fitting a quadratic function does better than either of the extremes of a first or a fifth order polynomial." (S005, 2012-11-26) "However, the problems that the two models suffer from are very different." (S005, 2012-11-26) "reducing the data’s dimension can also reduce the complexity of the hypothesis class considered" (S012, 2012-11-26).
- 2018. Newer operating split. Avoidable bias means make the model bigger. Variance means add data. With a decent regularizer, a bigger model is usually safe. The reliable move he names for that year is still a bigger network and more data. A trick that targets variance usually does nothing for bias. "If you have high avoidable bias, increase the size of your model" (S001, 2018) "If you include a well-designed regularization method, then you can usually safely increase the size of the model without increasing overfitting." (S001, 2018) "But one of the more reliable ways to improve an algorithm’s performance today is still to (i) train a bigger network and (ii) get more data." (S001, 2018) "This technique helps with variance problems, but it usually has no significant effect on bias." (S001, 2018) "We see from these examples that knowing the optimal error rate is helpful for guiding our next steps." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20. Newer, and it qualifies the scale slogan. Both scale and new algorithms matter, and the mix depends on the problem, including how close you already are to human level or to Bayes error. "I think both are important. And it's also problem dependent." (S003, 2020-02-20).
The 2018 book treats these definitions as informal, chosen to pick the next action rather than to match a statistician's formalism (S001).

## Dev and test sets
Sources: S001, S003, S004, S005.
Dev and test are a steering wheel. Their job is to point the team at the next change. They have to look like the future, they have to share one distribution, and they have to be large enough to see the gap you care about. Build the first version fast. Change it when it points the wrong way. The test set holds the final number. Decisions stay on the dev set.
- 2012-11-26: score every candidate on data it was not trained on. A hold-out around 30% is his typical choice when data is plentiful. Ten-fold cross validation is the typical choice when you cannot spare that much. The guarantee he cares about assumes train and test come from the same distribution. "This algorithm does not work." (S004, 2012-11-26) "cross validation set, and 30% is a typical choice." (S004, 2012-11-26) "A typical choice for the number of folds to use here would be k = 10." (S004, 2012-11-26) "assumption of training and testing on the same distribution, and the assumption of the independently drawn training examples, were the most important." (S005, 2012-11-26).
- 2018. Newer. Drop the old 70/30 habit once you have a lot of data. The dev and test sets can be a much smaller share, sized to the difference you need to detect (S001). Draw them from the data you expect to see, not from whatever you already collected. Keep them on one distribution. On a new application, ask for a first dev set, test set, and metric in under a week. That week does not apply to a mature product such as anti-spam. If dev and test, or the metric, start steering badly, change them. Three triggers he names: the real distribution has moved, you have overfit the dev set, or the metric ranks a worse system first. Never use the test set to choose an algorithm, including a decision to roll back. A dev set you stare at needs a blackbox slice you never look at, or you will overfit the slice you touch. Third-party benchmarks with mismatched dev and test reward luck. About 100 mistakes is enough to see the major sources. "the purpose of the dev and test sets are to direct your team toward the most important changes to make to the machine learning system" (S001, 2018) "Choose dev and test sets to reflect data you expect to get in the future and want to do well on." (S001, 2018) "don’t assume your training distribution is the same as your test distribution." (S001, 2018) "Having mismatched dev and test sets makes it harder to figure out what is and isn’t working, and thus makes it harder to prioritize what to work on." (S001, 2018) "I typically ask my teams to come up with an initial dev/test set and an initial metric in less than one week" (S001, 2018) "It is better to come up with something imperfect and get going quickly, rather than overthink this." (S001, 2018) "But this one week timeline does not apply to mature applications." (S001, 2018) "If you ever find that the dev/test sets or metric are no longer pointing your team in the right direction, it’s not a big deal! Just change them" (S001, 2018) "But do not use the test set to make any decisions regarding the algorithm, including whether to roll back to the previous week’s system." (S001, 2018) "The dev set should be large enough to detect differences between algorithms that you are trying out." (S001, 2018) "There is no need to have excessively large dev/test sets beyond what is needed to evaluate the performance of your algorithms." (S001, 2018) "Luck, rather than skill, will have a greater impact on your performance on such benchmarks" (S001, 2018) "If you see the performance on the Eyeball dev set improving much more rapidly than the performance on the Blackbox dev set, you have overfit the Eyeball dev set." (S001, 2018) "With ~100 mistakes, you would get a very good sense of the major sources of errors." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: a clean test score is the start of the problem, because the factory changes after you freeze the set. "it works well on your test set, but guess what? You know, something changes in the factory." (S003, 2020-02-20).

## Error analysis
Sources: S001, S002, S003.
Before a month of building, take about 100 mistakes and count them by hand. The share of errors in a category is a ceiling on what fixing that category can win. Dogs at 5% of errors cannot move the headline number much. Dogs at half of errors can. Engineers skip this because building feels better.
- 2018: "Manually examining 100 examples does not take long." (S001, 2018) "These two hours could save you a month of wasted effort." (S001, 2018) "I’ve seen many engineers reluctant to carry out error analysis." (S001, 2018) "you find that only 5% of the misclassified images are dogs, then no matter how much you improve your algorithm’s performance on dog images, you won’t get rid of more than 5% of your errors." (S001, 2018) "Before investing a month on this task, I recommend that you first estimate how much it will actually improve the system’s accuracy." (S001, 2018) "It often feels more exciting to just jump in and implement some idea, rather than question if the idea is worth the time investment." (S001, 2018) "Most machine learning problems leave clues that tell you what’s useful to try, and what’s not useful to try." (S001, 2018) "If you choose poorly, you might waste months." (S001, 2018) For a pipeline, attribute each mistake to the first part whose perfect output would have made the answer right. "Say you go through 100 misclassified dev set images and find that 90 of the errors are attributable to the cat detector, and only 10 errors are attributable to the cat breed classifier." (S001, 2018) If every part is near a person and the whole system is not, the pipeline is missing information. "The only possible conclusion is that the ML pipeline is flawed." (S001, 2018) When a system picks a max score, compare the score of the right answer with the score of what it output, so you know whether search or scoring is at fault. "Depending on which of these was the cause of the failure, you should prioritize your efforts very differently." (S001, 2018) The count is a guide. It does not hand you a formula, and it works best when a person can do the task. You will also overfit whatever slice you keep rereading, so look through a small window. "Error analysis does not produce a rigid mathematical formula that tells you what the highest priority task should be." (S001, 2018) "Many error analysis processes work best when we are trying to automate something humans can do and can thus benchmark against human-level performance." (S001, 2018) "You will more rapidly overfit the portion that you are manually looking at." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: people who can debug a model that is not learning are far faster, on the order of 10x and sometimes 100x. "I find that the people that are really good at debugging machine learning algorithms are easily 10x, maybe 100x faster at getting something to work." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: he ties the same habit to data-centric development by name. "I’ve found EDA particularly useful in data-centric AI development, where analyzing errors and gaining insights can really help drive progress!" (S002, 2022-12-13).
There is no guarantee the matching data exists once the count tells you what is missing (S001). The book is written for people shipping applications, not for people whose goal is a paper (S001).

## Pilots and the first system
Sources: S001, S002, S003.
Start smaller than the project you are excited about. Build a basic system in days and let its errors pick the direction. He has watched more companies fail by starting too big than by starting too small.
- 2018: do not design the perfect system first. The speed of the idea, code, experiment loop is the speed of progress. Even in anti-spam, a field he knew well, he would not have trusted himself to pick the direction up front. Experienced researchers try dozens of ideas before one is good enough. "So don’t start off trying to design and build the perfect system." (S001, 2018) "The faster you can go round this loop, the faster you will make progress." (S001, 2018) "Even though I have worked extensively on anti-spam, I would still have a hard time picking one of these directions." (S001, 2018) "Even experienced machine learning researchers will usually try out many dozens of ideas before they discover something satisfactory." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: the company version of the same rule is a small internal win before the giant project. At Google Brain that order was speech, then house numbers in Maps, then Ads. "I've seen a lot more companies fail by starting too big than by starting too small." (S003, 2020-02-20) "For a company to become good at machine learning, sometimes the right thing to do is not to tackle the giant project, is instead to do the small project that lets the organization learn and then build up from there." (S003, 2020-02-20) "But the first step is actually to start small." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13. Newer split by cost. When he was younger he executed the first idea that excited him. He now aims first when the shot is expensive or hard to undo and a study can show the value. He fires first when the shot is cheap, and he fires many times. Building the model inside a product is in the cheap bucket: get a working system up and revise it. Do not spend a month choosing a project that would take a week. "When I was younger, I used to execute on the first idea I was excited about." (S002, 2022-12-13) "Ready, Aim, Fire tends to be superior when the cost of execution is high and a study can shed light on how useful or valuable a project could be." (S002, 2022-12-13) "When taking a shot is inexpensive, it also makes sense to take many shots." (S002, 2022-12-13) "it’s often worth spending more time in advance to make sure it really is a good idea." (S002, 2022-12-13) "So it makes sense to build an end-to-end system quickly and revise it until it works well." (S002, 2022-12-13) "It doesn’t make sense to spend a month deciding whether to work on a project that would take a week to complete." (S002, 2022-12-13) "Don’t worry about starting too small." (S002, 2022-12-13).
A company pilot and a model experiment are the same shape at different prices (inferred from S001, S002, S003). His 2022 phrase, an end-to-end system, means a prototype you can run and revise. The 2018 warning still covers an end-to-end neural net when labeled pairs are scarce (inferred from S001, S002).

## End-to-end learning and the rest of the system
Sources: S001, S003.
Prefer a pipeline when you do not have a lot of labeled pairs for the whole task, or when you can break the job into simpler pieces you can label. He is skeptical of end-to-end driving until that data exists. The learned model is a small fraction of what you have to ship, and the work around it has to be redesigned.
- 2018: "When this type of data is not available, approach end-to-end learning with great caution." (S001, 2018) "Until more end-to-end data becomes available, I believe the non-end-to-end approach is significantly more promising for autonomous driving" (S001, 2018) "But I’m skeptical about end-to-end learning for autonomous driving." (S001, 2018) "Having more hand-engineered components generally allows a speech system to learn with less data." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: "the machine learning model is maybe 5% or even fewer relative to the entire software system you need to build." (S003, 2020-02-20) "So you need to redesign a lot of tasks around that thing you've now automated." (S003, 2020-02-20) "There's a huge gulf between something that works in a Jupyter notebook on your laptop versus something that runs in a production deployment setting" (S003, 2020-02-20).

## AI
Sources: S001, S002, S003.
He treats AI as a general purpose technology, on the order of electricity, that will change every industry, and he aims it at applications. It will not solve every problem. The part of the early deep-learning bet he stands by is scale. He also says that centralizing data and processing it concentrates power.
- 2018: the practical advice is for builders of applications. "This advice is meant for readers wanting to build AI applications, rather than those whose goal is to publish academic papers." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: "AI is a general purpose technology, and I think it will transform every industry." (S003, 2020-02-20) "The part we got right was the importance of scale." (S003, 2020-02-20) "I like stuff that works." (S003, 2020-02-20) "So machine learning is automation on steroids." (S003, 2020-02-20) "The AI and internet are causing an acceleration of concentration of power because we can now centralize data, use AI to process it." (S003, 2020-02-20) He is more motivated when he can see a line from the work to helping people, and he marks that as personal taste. "But I am more motivated when I can see a line to how the work that my teams and I are doing helps people." (S003, 2020-02-20) "and this is personal taste, I'm not saying anyone else should do what I do" (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: "AI is the new electricity. It will transform and improve all areas of human life." (S002, 2022-12-13) "AI won’t solve every problem, but as a community, let’s look for ways to make a positive impact wherever we can." (S002, 2022-12-13) "Uses of AI and data science can be found in almost any situation that produces data." (S002, 2022-12-13).
AGI timing, reinforcement learning as a product bet, and self-supervised learning as a piece are under Hype and under Which model to try.

## Culture
Sources: S001, S002, S003.
Culture is the people in the room and the priorities you repeat. A few changes in what the team works on first move output more than a slogan. He points the education teams at the learner.
- 2018: "The only thing better than being a superhero is being part of a superhero team." (S001, 2018) "A few changes in prioritization can have a huge effect on your team’s productivity." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: "We're all social creatures. We tend to become more like the people around us." (S003, 2020-02-20) "What matters the most is who are the 10 people, who are the 30 people you interact with every day." (S003, 2020-02-20) "the number one priority is to do what's best for learners, do what's best for students." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: "if most of your colleagues work hard, learn continuously, and build AI to benefit all people, you’re likely to do the same." (S002, 2022-12-13) "But the teammates you’ll work with are at least equally important." (S002, 2022-12-13) "I find that people who aim to lift others during every step of their own journey often achieve better outcomes for themselves." (S002, 2022-12-13).

## Metrics
Sources: S001, S002.
Give the team one number that can rank models. Turn every other goal into a pass or fail bar, such as maximize accuracy subject to a runtime cap. Pair that learning metric with a business metric. If you cannot name a milestone, you do not understand the problem yet. On a mature product, a tiny gain can be worth the work because it hits profit. He does not use significance tests to judge interim progress.
- 2018: "Having a single-number evaluation metric speeds up your ability to make a decision when you are selecting among a large number of classifiers." (S001, 2018) "Then, maximize accuracy, subject to your classifier meeting the running time criteria." (S001, 2018) "So, you can no longer trust the metric to pick the best algorithm." (S001, 2018) "I usually do not find statistical significance tests useful for measuring interim progress." (S001, 2018) "I have also seen teams that are highly motivated to eke out even a 0.01% improvement, since it has a direct impact on the company’s profits." (S001, 2018).
- 2022-12-13: "This includes both machine learning metrics (such as accuracy) and business metrics (such as revenue)." (S002, 2022-12-13) "Unfortunately, not every business problem can be reduced to optimizing test set accuracy!" (S002, 2022-12-13) "If you aren’t able to determine reasonable milestones, it may be a sign that you need to learn more about the problem." (S002, 2022-12-13).

## Train and dev mismatch, and extra data
Sources: S001, S003.
When training data and dev data come from different places, measure the gap on purpose. Extra data from another source is safe for a big flexible network when one input-to-label mapping fits both sources. It hurts when the same input deserves different labels, as with a house of a given size priced in two cities. Down-weight a large, different source instead of building a giant model to swallow it. Put some of the real user data into training.
- 2018: older practice warned against mixing sources. He says that risk has greatly diminished for a large flexible network, and it remains for a small one (S001). "Try to understand what properties of the data differ between the training and the dev set distributions." (S001, 2018) "If you only care about predicting New York City housing prices, putting the two datasets together will hurt your performance." (S001, 2018) "If you can afford to build a neural network with a large enough number of hidden units/layers, you can safely add the 20,000 images to your training set." (S001, 2018) "But if you do not have a big enough neural network (or another highly flexible learning algorithm), then you should pay more attention to your training data matching your dev/test set distribution." (S001, 2018) "By weighting the additional Internet images less, you don’t have to build as massive a neural network to make sure the algorithm does well on both types of tasks." (S001, 2018) "We can put the remaining 5,000 user-uploaded examples into the training set." (S001, 2018) "Unfortunately, there are no guarantees in this process." (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: a dramatic shift between train and test is still an open problem, which is why a factory deployment keeps breaking a laptop score. "we don't know how to deal with test set distributions that are dramatically different than the training set distribution." (S003, 2020-02-20).

## Human-level performance
Sources: S001, S003.
Use the best human performance you can actually get, a team of experts rather than a junior, as the stand-in for the best possible error. That number chooses bias work versus variance work. A reachable target speeds a team up. Progress usually slows once machines pass people. Problems people can do are easier to error-analyze and easier to label.
- 2018: "In this case, I would use 2% as the human-level performance proxy for our optimal error rate." (S001, 2018) "I find that having a reasonable and achievable target error rate helps accelerate a team’s progress." (S001, 2018) "progress is usually slower on problems where machines already surpass human-level performance, while progress is faster when machines are still trying to catch up to humans." (S001, 2018) "This is another advantage of working on problems that humans can solve" (S001, 2018).
- 2020-02-20: a few sets may already be near that ceiling. "So for a few data sets, we may be approaching your Bayes error rate, or approaching or surpassing human level performance." (S003, 2020-02-20).

## What AI project to start
Sources: S002, S003.
Start from a business problem. Ask a domain expert for the top three things they wish worked better, and why they do not yet, then brainstorm more than one approach and check feasibility and value before you staff it. Feasibility can be published work, what competitors have already done, or a quick proof. Value is a question for the operator who lives with the problem. Set a learning milestone and a business milestone, and budget data, people, time, and help from other teams. If a step shows the direction is empty, go back. It is acceptable to conclude that AI is the wrong tool.
- 2020-02-20: outside the consumer internet, many of those problems have few people on them. "there's so many opportunities that very few people are working on." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: "Identify a business problem (not an AI problem)." (S002, 2022-12-13) "Sometimes there isn’t a good AI solution, and that’s okay too." (S002, 2022-12-13) "But the variety of applications to which machine learning has not yet been applied is vast!" (S002, 2022-12-13) "How can you come up with a plan for building a system when you don’t know in advance how long it will take to achieve the target accuracy?" (S002, 2022-12-13) His worked case is a power grid that cannot predict wind and solar. The business problem comes first. Satellite images and weather data are candidate tools. Operators judge the value (S002).

## Hiring for AI
Sources: S002, S003.
He hires and joins for the people in the room, and for the skill of debugging a model that is not learning. A rotation that cannot name the manager and the peers is a worrying answer. A startup is an easier place than a big company to change roles. A PhD is one good path among several. Coursework is an efficient start. Defining your own problem and your own data is the harder step. Technical leadership, including the call to collect a certain kind of data, is not reserved for managers. This corpus does not give a scorecard, a compensation rule, or an interview loop.
- 2020-02-20: "I find that the people that are really good at debugging machine learning algorithms are easily 10x, maybe 100x faster at getting something to work." (S003, 2020-02-20) "What matters the most is who are the 10 people, who are the 30 people you interact with every day." (S003, 2020-02-20) "I think that's a worrying answer because it means you may not get sent to, you may not actually get to a team with great peers and great people to work with." (S003, 2020-02-20) "It's actually much harder if you need to go and define your own problem and find your own data set" (S003, 2020-02-20) "So coursework is actually a very efficient way for people to learn concepts in the beginning parts of breaking into a new field." (S003, 2020-02-20) "I think that there are multiple good options of which doing a PhD could be one of them." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: "If you’re considering a role switch, a startup can be an easier place to do it than a big company." (S002, 2022-12-13) "Leadership isn’t just for managers." (S002, 2022-12-13) "But the teammates you’ll work with are at least equally important." (S002, 2022-12-13).

## Deployment, drift, and maintenance
Sources: S002, S003.
A passing test on a laptop is the first step. Teams underestimate the rest: robustness when the lights, the background, or the process changes, the software around the model, who acts when the system flags a defect, and maintenance when you cannot page a bench of machine learning engineers. A speech outage can call on about 20 engineers. A factory outage cannot (S003). Plans are hard because you do not know when the accuracy target will be hit, and drift continues after it is hit. Tools for managing data are immature, and he thinks that gap is still full of useful work. He says the field still lacks terms for some of this operations work and is making them up as it goes (S003).
- 2020-02-20: "But a lot of teams underestimate the rest of the steps needed." (S003, 2020-02-20) "I think there is a gulf between what it takes to do well on a test set on your hard drive versus what it takes to work well in a deployment setting." (S003, 2020-02-20) "We're very immature in terms of tools for managing data" (S003, 2020-02-20) "I think there's a lot of innovation there to be had still." (S003, 2020-02-20) "but we don't even have systematic terminology yet to describe some of the stuff we do, because I think we're indenting it on the fly." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: "Even after the system has hit the target, further iteration may be necessary to address post-deployment drift." (S002, 2022-12-13) "How can you come up with a plan for building a system when you don’t know in advance how long it will take to achieve the target accuracy?" (S002, 2022-12-13).

## Timing
Sources: S002, S003.
Enter an application while few people are on it. Conviction before the idea is popular is how he explains the bets that mattered, including helicopters, online advertising, and scale. The result that later looked sudden had taken years. When the future is fuzzy, write a few scenarios and start the actions that are sane in more than one of them.
- 2020-02-20: "if you want to make a breakthrough, you sometimes have to have conviction and do something before it's popular, since that lets you have a bigger impact." (S003, 2020-02-20) "It's one of those overnight successes that took years to get there." (S003, 2020-02-20).
- 2022-12-13: "partly because I jumped in when relatively few people were working on those applications." (S002, 2022-12-13) In March 2020 he wrote three Covid recoveries and a plan for each. "I imagined quick (three months), medium (one year), and slow (two years) recoveries" (S002, 2022-12-13) He also likes pooling many experts into one probability, in the Superforecasting style (S002).
He gives no date for AGI. That is under Hype.

## Projects he will not build
Sources: S002, S003.

## Customers and what to build
Sources: S003.
Most of the startup failures he points at come from building something nobody wanted. The customer's vote is the one that lasts. Keep asking whether the customer would give a thumbs up. His studio's two checks are how many customer calls it takes to validate the demand, and whether the technology can actually be built. A result in a paper is a long way from a production system (S003). Ship a feature only after real users touch it. A shared-login feature for a San Francisco school, so two people could split credit, had zero users, because people watch alone.
- 2020-02-20: "In Silicon Valley, a lot of startup failures come from building our products that no one wanted." (S003, 2020-02-20) "I think startups that are very customer focused, customer obsessed, deeply understand the customer and are oriented to serve the customer, are more likely to succeed." (S003, 2020-02-20) "Ultimately, we don't get to vote if we succeed or fail." (S003, 2020-02-20) "So as you build a startup, you have to constantly ask the question, will the customer give a thumbs up on this?" (S003, 2020-02-20) "how many customer calls do we need to make in order to get customer validation? How do we make sure that this technology can be built?" (S003, 2020-02-20) "Zero people use this feature." (S003, 2020-02-20).

## Hype
Sources: S003.
Far-off catastrophe talk is a distraction from the failures you can point at today. He uses overpopulation on Mars as the joke for a real worry scheduled on the wrong day. Talking to regulators about a promise not to wipe out humanity is, for him, easier than facing the hard present problems, and he says so. The self-driving failure he wants solved first is a car that does not brake for a truck across the road. He will not estimate whether AGI is a hundred, five hundred, or five thousand years away. Self-supervised learning is a piece, not the whole solution. In February 2020, reinforcement learning was not what was working in products, and he left room for that to change.
- 2020-02-20: "I do worry about overpopulation on the planet Mars, just not today." (S003, 2020-02-20) "a huge distraction from the much harder problems that we actually need to address today" (S003, 2020-02-20) "Well, it's much more fun to talk to them about how you promised not to wipe out humanity than to face the actually really hard problems we face." (S003, 2020-02-20) "The biggest problem with self-driving cars is when there's a big white truck across the road" (S003, 2020-02-20) "but whether it takes a hundred years or 500 or 5,000, I find hard to estimate." (S003, 2020-02-20).

## Synthetic data
Sources: S001.
Synthetic data can close a mismatch, for example car noise mixed into quiet speech, or blur added to sharp images. It works only after the details match the real distribution, and his teams have spent weeks getting there. A set that looks real to a person can still be a narrow set a model memorizes: one hour of noise repeated across a thousand hours, noise from a handful of cars, or images from about 20 video-game car models (S001). Do not leave a tell that lets the model separate fake examples from real ones.
- 2018: "When synthesizing data, put some thought into whether you’re really synthesizing a representative set of examples." (S001, 2018) "it is sometimes easier to create synthetic data that appears realistic to a person than it is to create data that appears realistic to a computer." (S001, 2018) "my teams have sometimes taken weeks before we produced data with details that are close enough to the actual distribution for the synthesized data to have a significant effect." (S001, 2018) "Try to avoid giving the synthesized data properties that makes it possible for a learning algorithm to distinguish synthesized from non-synthesized examples" (S001, 2018).
