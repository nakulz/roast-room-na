# Books: Andrew Ng

Two books. Built from the notes on those books (S001 and S002) and from S003, where he uses the same decisions in conversation. The CS229 lecture notes (S004 to S016) are course notes, not a book, and they are not given an entry. Summaries are in my own words. Book text is not reproduced.

## Machine Learning Yearning (2018, draft)
- **Core thesis:** An applied machine learning team moves by reading clues in the errors and the data, then doing the next thing those clues justify. Most wasted months come from building an idea before counting whether it can move the error, from dev and test sets that do not match the future, and from reaching for more data or a fancier model by default.
- **Key frameworks:** Hand-count about 100 mistakes and treat each category's share as a ceiling. Split error into avoidable bias and variance using an optimal-error estimate, usually the best human team. Plot a learning curve before a data-collection project. Build dev and test sets from the future distribution, on one distribution, and change them when they point the wrong way. Rank models with one number and hold other goals to pass/fail bars. When train and dev differ, add a training-dev set and then match the data. Mix an extra dataset only when one input-to-label mapping fits, and down-weight a huge different source. Treat synthetic data as useful after it represents the real distribution, not after it looks real to a person. Prefer a pipeline over end-to-end learning when paired labels are scarce. Build a basic system in days and let the loop run. He uses a borrowed image of a finite attic, from another writer's detective story, to say a small network is hurt by useless extra data. That story is not his book.
- **Taught in sources:** The draft is S001. He wants teammates to read it so a few changes in priority raise the team's output. The same moves show up later in conversation (S003, 2020) and in the career ebook (S002, 2022): error analysis, a test before you collect data, and a basic system before a perfect design. The draft is aimed at people building applications. He sets academic publishing outside its scope. The one-week setup rule does not cover mature products. Error analysis is weakest when humans cannot do the task.
- **How his later notes sit beside it:** S003 (2020, newer) adds the deployment gulf, small-data label fights, and the admission that a dramatic distribution shift is still unsolved. S002 (2022, newer) adds an explicit business metric next to the ML metric. Neither cancels the 2018 mechanics.

## How to Build Your Career in AI (2022)
- **Core thesis:** Getting good at AI is a sequence of foundations, projects, and a job, with a community around the work. The part a founder uses is how to choose and run the project: start from a business problem, generate more than the first idea, check that it can be built and that it is worth it, set both a machine learning milestone and a business milestone, and budget data, people, time, and help from other teams.
- **Key frameworks:** Five steps to scope a project, ending in an acceptable stop when no good AI solution exists. Ready, Aim, Fire when the shot is expensive or hard to undo, and Ready, Fire, Aim when the shot is cheap, including while building the model. A ladder from small projects to projects that earn resources. Learn to the depth the next decision requires. As tools become turnkey, less math is enough to ship them, while bias, variance, and error analysis stay. Take only work that is good for people. He points at other writers for tiny habits and for pooling expert judgments into a probability. Those books are not his.
- **Taught in sources:** The ebook is S002, dated December 2022. It is short, and he says the real path is larger than one ebook. S003 (2020) already shows the company version of the same choices: a small first project, customer calls before a startup bet, and a thumbs-up test. In S002 he also drops an older personal habit, executing the first idea that excited him. The 2022 rule is the newer one.
- **How it sits beside Yearning:** Use Yearning to decide whether data, labels, or the model will move the error. Use this ebook to decide whether the project should exist, how big the first version is, and whether to study or to build. His phrase in this book, build an end-to-end system quickly, means a working slice you can revise. It does not overrule the 2018 caution against an end-to-end architecture when paired labels are scarce.
